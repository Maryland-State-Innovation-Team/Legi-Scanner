Health 
 
 
Pharmaceutical Drugs and Devices - Gifts to Health Care Professionals - 
Prohibition 
 
 
This bill prohibits a person that sells drugs or devices from giving or offering gifts – not 
including peer-reviewed medical literature – to a health care professional that have a total 
combined value of more than $50 in one calendar year. An individual who violates the bill 
is guilty of a misdemeanor and, on conviction, may be subject to a fine of up to 
$5,000 and/or imprisonment for up to six months. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  Several health occupations boards advise that any reporting or other 
requirements can be absorbed by existing budgeted resources. Penalty provisions are not 
anticipated to materially impact State revenues.     
 
Local Effect:  None.     
 
Small Business Effect:  Minimal.    
 
 
Analysis 
 
Bill Summary:  “Gift” means any payment, food, entertainment, travel, subscription, 
advance, service, or anything else of value provided to a health care professional, unless 
the health care professional reimburses the cost at fair market value. 
 
“Health care professional” means (1) an individual who is licensed or certified under the 
Health Occupations Article and authorized to prescribe or recommend prescribed products; 
(2) a partnership or corporation composed of the aforementioned individuals; or (3) an 
officer, employee, agent, or contractor of a person described above who is acting in the

Current Law:  “Device” means any instrument, apparatus, or contrivance, or any part of 
accessory of an instrument, apparatus, or contrivance, that is intended (1) for use in the 
diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of human disease, or (2) to affect the 
structure or any function of the human body for medical, surgical, or therapeutic purposes. 
 
“Drug” means any substance or component of a substance (1) that is recognized in an 
official compendium; (2) that is intended for use in the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, 
treatment, or prevention of disease in human beings; or (3) except for food, that is intended 
to affect the structure or any function of the human body.  
 
There are no current laws or regulations regarding gifts to health care professionals. 
However, members of the Prescription Drug Affordability Board, established under 
Chapter 692 of 2019, are prohibited from accepting any gift or donation of services or 
property that indicates a potential conflict of interest or has the appearance of biasing the 
work of the board.  
 
Additionally, the federal Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act requires drug and 
medical device manufacturers to annually report gifts and payments made to physicians 
and teaching hospitals.
