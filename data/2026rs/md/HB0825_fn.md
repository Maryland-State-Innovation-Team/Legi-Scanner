Brain Health - Guidelines, Grant Fund, and Commemorative Month (Tahjai's 
Law) 
 
 
This bill creates the Maryland Brain Health Grant Fund, administered by the Maryland 
Department of Health (MDH), to provide grants to nonprofits, health care providers, local 
health departments (LHDs), and community‑based programs to support brain health 
awareness, brain health screening, brain illness prevention, and brain health services. In 
fiscal 2028, the Governor must include $500,000 in the annual budget bill for the fund. 
MDH must develop specified guidelines and recommendations related to brain health 
evaluation and submit related reports. The Governor must proclaim May as Brain Health 
and Chronic Headache Awareness Month. The bill takes effect July 1, 2026. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  MDH general fund expenditures increase by $500,000 annually beginning 
in FY 2027 to fund the Brain Health Grant Fund. MDH special fund revenues and 
expenditures increase accordingly to hire staff and distribute grants. General fund revenues 
increase minimally beginning in FY 2027 for any interest earned by the special fund. This 
bill establishes a mandated appropriation for FY 2028.

Analysis 
 
Bill Summary:  
 
Brain Health Grant Fund 
 
“Brain health services” include prevention, evaluation, diagnosis, treatment, therapy, 
education, and support related to a brain tumor, chronic headache, migraine, traumatic 
brain injury, cerebrovascular condition, and any other neurological pain disorder.  
 
The fund consists of money appropriated in the State budget to the fund and any other 
money from any other source accepted for the benefit of the fund. Any interest earnings 
must be credited to the general fund. Expenditures may only be made in accordance with 
the State budget, and the fund can only be used for: 
 
 
providing grants to nonprofit organizations, health care providers, LHDs, and 
community-based programs to support (1) brain health awareness activities, 
(2) brain health screening services, (3) brain illness prevention activities, and 
(4) brain health services;  
 
administrative expenses of MDH in administering the fund; and 
 
any other use determined to be appropriate by MDH after consultation with The 
Solid Foundation, Inc., relevant clinical experts, and patient advocacy groups.  
 
MDH must periodically consult with stakeholders, including the Solid Foundation, Inc., 
regarding use of the fund. MDH must also (1) establish transparent criteria for awarding 
grants from the fund, (2) prioritize providing grants to support activities in underserved 
communities, and (3) ensure equitable geographic distribution of grants throughout the 
State.  
 
Guidelines and Recommendations 
 
MDH must collaborate with stakeholders to develop guidelines that promote the 
incorporation of a risk-based brain health evaluation into a routine physical examination 
and wellness visit. An evaluation may include a neurological assessment, an 
evidence‑based decision tool, and a referral protocol for noninvasive imaging.

MDH must also develop recommendations (1) establishing screening intervals for brain 
health evaluations and (2) promoting insurance coverage parity for medically necessary 
brain health diagnostic tools.  
 
Brain Health and Chronic Headache Awareness Month 
 
The designation of May as Brain Health and Chronic Headache Awareness Month must 
align with national Go Gray in May brain tumor awareness initiatives. 
 
Reports 
 
By December 1, 2026, and annually thereafter, MDH must submit a report to the Governor 
and the General Assembly that includes statewide data for the immediately preceding 
fiscal year on (1) brain tumor and chronic headache prevalence; (2) emergency department 
visits related to brain tumors and chronic headaches; (3) access to diagnostic brain imaging; 
and (4) barriers to brain health-related care. 
 
By December 1, 2026, and annually thereafter, MDH must submit a report to the Governor 
and the General Assembly on the use of the Maryland Brain Health Grant Fund during the 
immediately preceding fiscal year. 
 
By January 1, 2027, MDH must submit a report to the Governor and the General Assembly 
regarding the specified guidelines and recommendations related to brain health evaluations 
during routine physical examinations. 
 
Current Law:  Chapters 548 and 549 of 2011 required the Maryland State Department of 
Education to collaborate with MDH to develop policies and implement a program to 
provide concussion awareness to coaches, school personnel, students, and parents. 
Once removed from play, a student or youth athlete may not return to play until he or she 
has obtained written clearance from a licensed health care professional trained in the 
evaluation and management of concussions.  
 
Chapter 731 of 2022 established the Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder and Traumatic Brain 
Injury Alternative Therapies Fund to support MDH in studying the effectiveness of and 
improving access to alternative therapies for post-traumatic stress disorder and traumatic 
brain injuries in veterans.   
 
May is National Brain Tumor Awareness month.

State Revenues:  
 
General Funds  
 
As any interest earnings from the special fund must accrue to the general fund, general 
fund revenues increase minimally beginning in FY 2027. 
 
Special Funds 
 
Special fund revenues to the Brain Health Grant Fund increase by $500,000 in fiscal 2028 
to reflect the bill’s mandated appropriation. To the extent discretionary funding is provided 
in fiscal 2027, fiscal 2029, and beyond, special fund revenues increase indeterminately. As 
the fund is permanent and the bill requires annual reporting on the use of the fund beginning 
December 1, 2026, this analysis assumes discretionary funding is provided beginning in 
fiscal 2027 and continues in fiscal 2029 and beyond (after the fiscal 2028 mandated 
appropriation). 
 
State Expenditures:   
 
General Funds 
 
General fund expenditures increase by $500,000 in fiscal 2028 to reflect the bill’s 
mandated appropriation. As noted above, this analysis assumes discretionary funding is 
provided in fiscal 2027, fiscal 2029, and out‑years.  
 
Special Funds 
 
MDH advises that it requires two additional staff to implement the bill, specifically 
one part-time community health educator to administer the fund and one part-time 
epidemiologist to conduct data analysis and work on the guidelines and recommendations, 
as well as a $50,000 annual contract in fiscal 2027 through 2029 to provide expertise in 
developing the guidelines and recommendations. 
 
The Department of Legislative Services advises that one full-time health policy analyst 
would be better suited to handle the responsibilities. Given that the guidelines and 
recommendations must be completed by January 1, 2027, the staff responsible for that work 
can continue to conduct data analysis to submit annual reports and manage the grant 
program. Additionally, though medical expertise is necessary to develop the guidelines and 
recommendations, the bill requires collaboration with practitioners and other stakeholders 
that can provide such expertise.
