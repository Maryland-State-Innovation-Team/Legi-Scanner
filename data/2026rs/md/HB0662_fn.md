Election Law - Election Judges - Compensation for Public School Students 
 
 
This bill authorizes a public school student serving as an election judge to both (1) earn 
hours toward the service-learning requirement for graduation under State Board of 
Education regulations and (2) receive the applicable election judge compensation. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  State finances are not expected to be materially affected. 
  
Local Effect:  Local government expenditures are expected to increase relatively 
minimally, per county, beginning in FY 2027, as discussed below. Revenues are not 
affected. 
  
Small Business Effect:  None. 
  
 
Analysis 
 
Current Law: 
 
Election Judge Compensation and Training 
 
For service as an election judge, a public school student in the State may (1) earn hours 
toward the service-learning requirement for graduation under State Board of Education 
regulations or (2) receive the applicable election judge compensation. A minor who is at 
least age 16 and who is a registered voter may be appointed and serve as an election judge. 
 
The compensation for each election judge who is not a returning election judge must be at 
least $250 per day for each Election Day and each early voting day actually served. For

each election judge who is a returning election judge, the compensation for each 
Election Day and each early voting day actually served must be at least $100 more per day 
than the compensation provided to an election judge who is not a returning election judge. 
“Returning election judge” means an election judge who has served a term of office for the 
immediately preceding statewide election. 
 
The State Board of Elections (SBE) is required to develop (and oversee the implementation 
of), in consultation with the local boards of elections, a program of instruction of 
election judges. Each local board must conduct election judge training based on the 
program developed by SBE, and each election judge, unless appointed under emergency 
circumstances, must participate in the program. A local board must pay an election judge 
at least $50 for each training the election judge completes, only after the election judge 
actually serves an Election Day or early voting day. 
 
Student Service-learning Requirement 
 
Pursuant to State Board of Education regulations, in order to graduate, students must 
complete (1) 75 hours of student service that includes preparation, action, and reflection 
components and that, at the discretion of the local school board system, may begin during 
the middle grades or (2) a locally designed program in student service that has been 
approved by the State Superintendent of Schools. 
 
Local Fiscal Effect:  Local government expenditures are expected to increase relatively 
minimally, per county, beginning in fiscal 2027 to provide compensation to public school 
students who serve as election judges and, in the absence of the bill, choose to earn hours 
toward the service-learning requirement and do not receive compensation.  
 
Information on the number of public school students, statewide, who have served as 
election judges in recent elections and chosen to earn hours toward the service-learning 
requirement is not readily available. However, for illustrative purposes, if the number of 
public school students who served as election judges in the 2024 general election and chose 
to earn hours toward the service-learning requirement in one county (44 students, in 
Montgomery County) is extrapolated statewide (in proportion to the level of voter 
registration in the counties), and it is assumed that each works one early voting day or 
Election Day, is paid the base salary of $250 and $50 for training, and is not a returning 
election judge (that receives higher compensation), local government expenditures 
collectively increase by approximately $81,000 per election to provide compensation to 
public school students serving as an election judge who otherwise choose to earn hours 
toward the service-learning requirement (and do not receive compensation) in the absence 
of the bill.
