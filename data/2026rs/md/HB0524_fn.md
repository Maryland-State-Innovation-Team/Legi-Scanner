General Provisions - Commemorative Months - African Heritage Month 
 
   
This bill requires the Governor to annually proclaim the month of September as 
African Heritage Month in recognition of the contributions that African Americans have 
made to the State. The proclamation must urge educational and cultural organizations to 
observe African Heritage Month with appropriate programs, ceremonies, and activities. 
The bill takes effect July 1, 2026. 
   
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  None. Commemorating African Heritage Month does not affect State 
finances. 
  
Local Effect:  None.      
  
Small Business Effect:  None.     
  
 
Analysis 
 
Current Law:  Twenty official commemorative days, seven months, and one week are 
recognized in State law, as shown in Appendix – Official Commemorative Days and 
Months in Maryland. The Governor also must issue a proclamation each year 
encouraging citizens and other individuals to observe a moment of silence at 3:00 p.m. on 
Memorial Day to unite in remembrance and commemorate the heroic acts and efforts of 
Marylanders who have served and died in the U.S. Armed Forces.
