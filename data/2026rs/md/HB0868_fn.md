Baltimore City - Public Middle Schools - Instruction on the Consequences of a 
Conviction for Felony Murder 
 
 
This bill requires the Baltimore City Board of School Commissioners to develop  
age-appropriate curriculum resources on the consequences of a conviction for felony 
murder for Baltimore City Public Schools (BCPS) students in grades 6 through 8. The 
board must determine the appropriate subject area in which to incorporate the instruction. 
Beginning in the 2027-2028 school year, the board must implement instruction based on 
the developed curriculum resources and the chief academic officer must ensure that each 
student receives the instruction at least once during grades 6 through 8. The bill takes 
effect July 1, 2026. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  None. This bill pertains only to BCPS. 
  
Local Effect:  BCPS expenditures may increase in FY 2027 to develop age-appropriate 
curriculum resources on the consequences of a conviction for felony murder. Beginning in 
FY 2028 (2027-2028 school year), BCPS expenditures may increase to incorporate the 
required instruction into an existing subject area and ensure that each student receives the 
instruction at least once during grades 6 through 8. Revenues are not affected. This bill 
may impose a mandate on a unit of local government. 
  
Small Business Effect:  None. 
  
 
Analysis 
 
Bill Summary:  “Consequences of a conviction for felony murder” is defined as the legal 
penalties that take away an individual’s freedom, rights, and access to programs or services 
after a conviction for murder in the first degree under Title 2 of the Criminal Law Article.

Current Law:  With the advice of the State Superintendent of Schools, the State Board of 
Education (SBE) establishes basic policy and guidelines for the program of instruction for 
public schools. Subject to State law and the regulations, bylaws, policies, and guidelines 
established by SBE, each local board of education must establish the curriculum guides 
and courses of study for schools in its jurisdiction. Policies, rules, and regulations for the 
graduation of students from Maryland public schools are established by local boards of 
education and SBE. 
 
The Maryland State Department of Education advises that at the middle school level the 
State Standards and Frameworks in Social Studies do not include the content required by 
the bill; however, opportunities to learn about criminal law and the criminal justice system 
are directly addressed in High School United States History and American Government. 
 
Local Expenditures:  The bill requires BCPS to develop age-appropriate curriculum 
resources and incorporate instruction on the consequences of a conviction for felony 
murder, beginning in the 2027-2028 school year. Each student must be taught the 
curriculum at least once in grades 6 through 8. It is assumed that, under the direction of the 
Baltimore City Board of School Commissioners, BCPS develops the curriculum resources 
and trains teachers in fiscal 2027 and 2028 to ensure implementation by the  
2027-2028 school year. As a result, BCPS expenditures for curriculum development are 
expected to increase during both fiscal 2027 and 2028. There may also be costs associated 
with tracking that each student receives the instruction at least once during  
grades 6 through 8. The likely costs cannot be reliably estimated but may be meaningful. 
 
Additional Comments:  In December 2021, the Department of Legislative Services 
produced a report entitled Collateral Consequences of a Criminal Conviction.
