5 (a) (1) IN THIS SECTION, THE FOLLOWING WORDS HAVE THE MEANINGS

7 (2) (I) “LIVING SHORELINE” MEANS A PROJECT TO MITIGATE
8 THE EFFECTS OF EROSION CAUSED BY SHORELINE FLOODING OR INUNDATION,
9 CURRENTS, AND WAVE ENERGY THROUGH PROJECT DESIGN THAT:

10 1. STABILIZES A SHORELINE BY USING NATURAL
11 MATERIALS TO CREATE BUFFERS TO ABSORB THE IMPACT OF COASTAL STORMS,
12 FLOODING, AND WAVE ENERGY AND TO PREVENT OR MINIMIZE SHORELINE
13 EROSION;

14 2. INCORPORATES AS MANY NATURAL ELEMENTS AS
15 POSSIBLE TO ENSURE A SUBSTANTIAL BIOLOGICAL COMPONENT, SUCH AS
16 NATIVE, TIDAL, OR LACUSTRINE FRINGE WETLANDS, OYSTER REEFS,
17 SUBMERGED AQUATIC VEGETATION, OR NATIVE PLANTS;

18 3. UTILIZES TECHNIQUES THAT INCORPORATE
19 ECOLOGICAL AND COASTAL ENGINEERING PRINCIPLES IN SHORELINE
20 STABILIZATION; AND

21 4. TO THE EXTENT POSSIBLE, MAINTAINS OR
22 RESTORES EXISTING NATURAL SLOPES AND CONNECTIONS BETWEEN UPLANDS
23 AND ADJACENT WETLANDS OR SURFACE WATERS.

24 (II) “LIVING SHORELINE” INCLUDES THE USE OF:

25 1. NATURAL ELEMENTS SUCH AS SAND, WETLAND
26 PLANTS, WOODY DEBRIS, OYSTERS OR OTHER SHELLFISH, SUBMERGED AQUATIC
27 VEGETATION, NATIVE GRASSES, SHRUBS, TREES, OR OTHER BIODEGRADABLE
28 MATERIALS AS APPROVED BY THE DEPARTMENT; AND

29 2. LOW PROFILE STRUCTURAL ELEMENTS
30 CHANNELWARD OF THE MEAN HIGH WATER LINE, SUCH AS STONE, CONCRETE,
31 OYSTER DOMES, OR OTHER APPROVED ENGINEERED STRUCTURES.

32 (3) (I) “NONSTRUCTURAL SHORELINE STABILIZATION
33 MEASURE” MEANS AN EROSION CONTROL MEASURE THAT IS:

34 1. DOMINATED BY TIDAL WETLAND VEGETATION; AND
