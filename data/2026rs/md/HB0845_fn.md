Higher Education - Student Financial Assistance - Alterations to Scholarship 
Programs 
 
 
This bill extends eligibility for the Maryland Community College Promise (MCCP) 
Scholarship to residents of Delmar, Maryland who graduate from Delmar High School in 
Delaware. The bill also expands eligibility for the Fallen Transportation Workers 
Scholarship Program to include specified surviving transportation workers. Acccordingly, 
the bill adjusts the name of the scholarship, the name of the associated special fund, and 
the statutory program purpose. The bill also expands the relationships to fallen and 
surviving transportation workers that qualify an individual for eligibility, as discussed 
below. Lastly, eligible uses for the Fallen Transportation Workers Scholarship are 
expanded to include the total cost of attendance at any institution of postsecondary 
education. The bill takes effect July 1, 2026. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  General fund expenditures and special fund revenues and expenditures at 
MHEC increase beginning as early as fiscal 2027 for additional awards made under the 
expanded Fallen and Surviving Transportation Workers Scholarship Program. 
Expenditures may also increase to the extent the authorization for scholarships to be used 
for total costs of attendance increases award amounts under the program. MHEC and 
institutions of higher education can make any needed administrative adjustments 
associated with the transportation scholarship with existing resources. Expanding 
eligibility for MCCP does not require additional spending; however, without additional 
spending, more individuals may be added to the waiting list.  
  
Local Effect:  Local community colleges can alter MCCP eligibility criteria on 
applications as needed using existing resources. Local community colleges can likely 
identify additional students eligible for the expanded Fallen and Surviving Transportation 
Workers Scholarship Program with existing resources. Revenues are not affected.

Analysis 
 
Bill Summary:   
 
Program Purpose 
 
Under the bill, the purpose of the Fallen and Surviving Transportation Workers Scholarship 
Program is to provide tuition assistance to a student who was impacted by an accident 
involving a fallen transportation worker or a surviving transportation worker. 
 
Definitions 
 
Under the bill, “surviving transportation worker” means an individual whose occupation is 
in the construction, rehabilitation, or operation of a transportation facility or transportation 
facilities project (as defined under existing provisions of the Transportation Article) in the 
State and who survived, on or after January 1, 2022, an accident resulting in at least  
$100 million in total damages to a transportation facility or transportation facilities project 
in the State and occurring while the individual was performing any job duty necessary for 
the construction, maintenance, rehabilitation, or operation of a transportation facility or 
transportation facilities project in the State. The bill also defines “unmarried partner” as an 
individual who, at the time of the fallen transportation worker’s death or surviving 
transportation worker’s accident, was in a committed relationship with the fallen or 
surviving transportation worker and shared a primary residence with the fallen 
transportation worker. 
 
Eligibility 
 
Under current law, an individual who is a child, stepchild, or surviving spouse of a fallen 
transportation worker may apply for a scholarship at an institution of postsecondary 
education (provided they meet other eligibility criteria). The bill expands eligibility to also 
include the following individuals (provided they meet the same other eligibility criteria 
specified in current law for dependents and spouses of fallen transportation workers):  
 
 
a surviving transportation worker; 
 
a child, stepchild, or spouse of a surviving transportation worker  
 
an unmarried partner of fallen and surviving transportation workers; and  
 
a child or stepchild of an unmarried partner of fallen and surviving transportation 
workers

Current Law:   
 
Maryland Community College Promise Scholarship 
 
Chapter 554 of 2018 created a scholarship program in Maryland to provide tuition 
assistance to students who attend a community college in the State and who meet specified 
eligibility criteria, including an income limitation. The MCCP Scholarship is a last-dollar 
award that is applied after State or federal non-loan aid to eligible recipients has been 
applied; an award cannot exceed $5,000 annually or actual tuition, whichever is less. 
Chapter 464 of 2019 specified that, if a local community college offers a Promise 
Scholarship, an award from the statewide program must be applied before the award of the 
local scholarship. 
 
To be eligible for the program, a student must: 
 
 
be eligible for in-state tuition, have graduated high school or completed a GED in 
the State, and enroll at a community college, in an eligible program; 
 
with certain exemptions, have earned at least a 2.3 high school GPA at the end of 
the first semester or the end of the senior year unless the applicant graduated from 
high school five or more years before the date of application, for an initial award, 
or have earned at least a 2.5 GPA while enrolled at a community college in the State; 
and 
 
have a gross adjusted income of $100,000 or less if the applicant is single or resides 
in a single-parent household or of $150,000 or less if married or resides in a 
two-parent household.

An applicant also has to attend the applicant’s local community college, except under

apprenticeship at a community college in the State. Further, an applicant must enroll in at

Under Chapters 377 and 378 of 2025, each recipient of a MCCP Scholarship may hold the 
award until the earlier of (1) three years after first enrolling as a candidate for a noncredit 
vocational certificate, in a sequence of credit or noncredit courses leading to licensure or 
certification, in credit or noncredit courses that are required for participation in a registered 
apprenticeship program, as a candidate for a certificate, or as a full-time candidate for an 
associate’s degree at a community college in the State; (2) six years after fist enrolling as 
a part-time candidate for an associate’s degree at a community college in the State; or 
(3) the date that the individual is awarded an associate’s degree. 
 
The Governor must appropriate at least $13.5 million annually for the program. Under 
Chapter 634 of 2023, each community college in the State, rather than the Maryland Higher 
Education Commission (MHEC) Office of Student Financial Assistance (OSFA), is 
responsible for the selection and distribution of awards. Nevertheless, annually, OSFA 
must allocate MCCP Scholarship funding to each community college in the State based on 
the proportion of Pell Grant-eligible students at each community college. If a community 
college does not award all funding allocated, the community college must distribute the 
remaining funding to other community colleges to make MCCP Scholarship awards in the 
award year. 
 
Initial awards are made based on greatest demonstrated need. Eligible applicants who do 
not receive an award are placed on a waiting list. For non-initial awards, priority must be 
given to prior-year recipients who remain eligible for the program as well as all eligible 
students who are enrolled in either at least 12 credits per semester or a sequence of credit 
or noncredit courses that lead to licensure or certification. Then, if any funds remain to be 
allocated, students enrolled in at least 6 credits per semester may be awarded scholarships. 
A recipient can receive the award for a total of three years (or up to six years for a part-time 
student). To maintain the award, the recipient must complete 6 credits per semester or make 
satisfactory progress toward a vocational certificate and earn a cumulative GPA of at 
least 2.5. The recipient must meet the designated income requirements for the program, 
remain eligible for in-state tuition, apply annually, and continue to make satisfactory 
academic progress as determined by the community college. 
 
MHEC must contact each school counselor at each public high school in the State with 
information on the MCCP Scholarship, including eligibility, availability, and deadlines for 
the scholarship. In addition, MHEC and each community college must post this

Fallen Transportation Workers Scholarship Program 
 
On March 26, 2024, the Francis Scott Key Bridge collapsed after being struck by a 
container vessel. In response to the incident, the Maryland General Assembly passed the 
PORT Act, Chapters 2 and 3 of 2024, to provide for a variety of relief measures relating to 
the collapse and resulting impacts to the operations of the Port of Baltimore. Chapters 2 
and 3 also established the Fallen Transportation Workers Scholarship Program to provide 
tuition assistance to students who were dependents of or are surviving spouses of a fallen 
transportation worker.  
 
“Fallen transportation worker” means an individual whose occupation is in the 
construction, rehabilitation, or operation of a transportation facility or transportation 
facilities project (as defined under existing provisions of the Transportation Article) in the 
State and who died on or after January 1, 2022, as a result of an accident occurring while 
the individual was performing any job duty necessary for the construction, maintenance, 
rehabilitation, or operation of a transportation facility or transportation facilities project in 
the State. To the extent practicable, the Maryland Department of Transportation (MDOT), 
a local department of transportation, or a contractor that employed fallen transportation 
workers must provide to MHEC the names and contact information for the families of the 
fallen transportation workers.  
 
 
Eligibility 
 
An individual who was a dependent of or is the surviving spouse of a fallen transportation 
worker may apply for a scholarship at an institution of postsecondary education. To apply, 
an individual must be (1) accepted for admission or enrolled in an undergraduate, graduate, 
or professional program at an institution of postsecondary education; (2) at least 
16 years old; and (3) the child, stepchild, or surviving spouse of a fallen transportation 
worker. Each institution of postsecondary education must determine the eligibility of 
individuals who apply to the institution for the Fallen Transportation Workers Scholarship 
Program.  
 
 
Scholarship Awards 
 
Scholarship awards may be used to cover tuition and mandatory fees at any institution of 
postsecondary education, but they may not exceed the equivalent annual tuition and 
mandatory fees of an undergraduate student at a four-year institution within the University 
System of Maryland (USM) (other than the University of Maryland, Baltimore Campus

Special Fund 
 
The Fallen Transportation Workers Scholarship Fund is established as a special, 
nonlapsing fund administered by MHEC. The fund consists of (1) money appropriated in 
the State budget to the fund; (2) interest earnings of the fund; and (3) any other money from 
any other source accepted for the benefit of the fund. Funding for the program is as 
provided in the State budget. 
 
State Expenditures:   
 
Maryland Community College Promise Scholarship 
 
MHEC advises that Maryland residents who graduate from Delmar High School are 
ineligible for a MCCP scholarship under current law. Although these students are eligible 
for in-State tuition, they did not graduate a high school in the State because Delmar High 
School is located in Delmar, Delaware. Students who live in Delmar, Maryland have 
historically been permitted to attend Delmar High School. 
 
Expanding eligibility for the MCCP scholarship does not alter the overall level of 
State funding for the program, which is set in the State operating budget the Budget 
Reconciliation and Financing Act (BRFA) of 2025 (Chapter 604) reduced subject to the 
requirement that the Governor must appropriate at least $13.5 million annually. Prior to 
fiscal 2026, the program was mandated to receive $15 million annually, however the 
amount that the Governor must annually provide from $15.0 million to $13.5 million 
beginning in fiscal 2026. The fiscal 2026 budget included $13.5 million for the program. 
The fiscal 2027 budget as passed by the General Assembly includes a $2.0 million 
reduction to the fiscal 2026 amount to align the appropriation with actual spending levels 
for the scholarship.

Fallen Transportation Workers Scholarship Program 
 
General fund expenditures increase beginning as early as fiscal 2027 to the extent funds 
are appropriated to the Fallen and Surviving Transportation Workers Scholarship Fund to 
fund scholarship awards to eligible students. Special fund revenues to and expenditures 
from the Fallen and Surviving Transportation Workers Scholarship Fund increase 
accordingly. The total number of additional awards likely to be awarded under an expanded 
program is unknown. The amount of such expenditures depends on the number and timing 
of scholarship awards. One transportation worker survived Francis Scott Key Bridge 
collapse, however the total number of additional unmarried partners, dependents of 
unmarried partners, and siblings of all fallen and surviving transportation workers remains 
unknown at this time.  
 
As discussed above, the maximum amount of each scholarship award is based on the 
equivalent annual tuition and mandatory fees of an undergraduate student at a four-year 
institution within USM (other than UMB or UMGC) with the highest annual expenses for 
a full-time undergraduate at the time the award is made. While a precise estimate remains 
infeasible at this time, the Department of Legislative Services notes that the scholarship 
expansion likely applies to a limited number of individuals and, depending on the ages of 
eligible individuals, program expenditures may be delayed several years and spread over 
at least 20 years.  
 
MHEC advises that the Fallen Transportation Works Scholarship has not made any awards 
to date. However, for the 2026-2027 academic year, eligible individuals may apply to their 
institution for the scholarship by July 15, 2026. Given the short timeframe between the 
bill’s July 1 effective date and July 15 application deadline, any new scholarship awards 
under the bill are likely to begin with the 2027-2028 academic year in fiscal 2028, though 
additional awards in fiscal 2027 remain possible. 
 
To the extent the authorization for awards to be used for the total cost of attendance at 
universities increases award sizes, general fund expenditures increase. Although, awards 
under the bill still may not exceed the equivalent annual tuition and mandatory fees of a 
full-time undergraduate Maryland resident at a four-year institution within USM – other 
than UMB and UMGC – with the highest annual expenses for a full-time resident 
undergraduate.

Local Fiscal Effect:  The bill does not alter the funding amount or distribution processes 
for MCCP scholarships. Each community college (including State-funded Baltimore City 
Community College) continues to receive a portion of the MCCP allocation in the 
State budget based on a calculation of each college’s Pell Grant-eligible population. 
 
Additional Comments:  MHEC advises that the bill’s language authorizing scholarships 
to be used for the “total cost of attendance” may result in financial control concerns. 
Specifically, MHEC advises that, since expenses beyond the direct cost of attendance are 
generally processed as direct refunds to students, these transactions are difficult to audit to 
confirm whether a student appropriately utilized the funds for true educational expenses.
