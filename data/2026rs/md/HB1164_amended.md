3 Article – Public Utilities

4 1–101.

5 (a) In this division the following words have the meanings indicated.

6 (z) (1) “Public service company” means a common carrier company, electric
7 company, gas company, sewage disposal company, telegraph company, telephone company,
8 water company, or any combination of public service companies.

9 (2) “Public service company” does not include:

10 (i) a campground that provides water, electric, gas, sewage, or
11 telephone service to campers incident to the campground’s primary business of operating
12 and maintaining the campground; or

13 (ii) a person that owns or operates equipment used for charging
14 electric vehicles, including a person that owns or operates:

15 1. an electric vehicle charging station;

16 2. electric vehicle supply equipment; or

17 3. an electric vehicle charging station service company or
18 provider.

19 (gg) “Sewage disposal company” means a privately owned public service company
20 that owns or maintains facilities for the disposal of sewage.

21 (uu) “Water company” means a public service company that owns a water plant
22 and sells or distributes water for gain.

23 4–309.

24 (a) (1) In this section the following words have the meanings indicated.

25 (2) “Eligible limited–income customer” means a residential customer of a
26 utility company with annual income that:

27 (i) 1. is at or below 175% of the federal poverty level; or

28 2. for a customer at least 67 years of age, is at or below 200%
29 of the federal poverty level; or

30 (ii) meets a broader designation approved by the Commission.

1 (3) “Limited–income mechanism” or “mechanism” means a process
2 approved by the Commission under this section to benefit an eligible limited–income
3 customer of a utility company.

4 (4) “Payment plan” means an agreement between an eligible
5 limited–income customer and a utility company to pay an arrearage balance over a specific
6 period of time to avoid disconnection of a utility service.

7 (5) (i) “Utility company” means an electric company, a gas and electric company, or a gas company.

8 (ii) “Utility company” does not include a small rural electric cooperative.

9 (b) The General Assembly finds and declares that the societal benefits of a
10 well–constructed limited–income mechanism to benefit Maryland’s eligible limited–income
11 customers are in the public interest.

12 (c) (1) Subject to the approval of the Commission, a utility company shall
13 adopt a limited–income mechanism to benefit an eligible limited–income customer.

14 (2) Notwithstanding § 4–503(b) of this title, the mechanism may take the
15 form of a program, tariff provision, credit, rate, rider, or other means to assist an eligible
16 limited–income customer to afford a utility service.

17 (3) A municipal electric utility may adopt a limited–income mechanism
18 subject to the approval of the Commission in the same manner as a utility company in
19 accordance with this section.

20 (4) THE COMMISSION MAY REQUIRE A WATER COMPANY, SEWAGE
21 DISPOSAL COMPANY, OR WATER AND SEWAGE DISPOSAL COMPANY TO ADOPT A
22 LIMITED–INCOME MECHANISM SUBJECT TO THE APPROVAL OF THE COMMISSION
23 IN THE SAME MANNER AS A UTILITY COMPANY IN ACCORDANCE WITH THIS
24 SECTION.

25 (d) (1) A utility company that proposes a limited–income mechanism for
26 Commission approval under subsection (c) of this section shall include the proposal in:

27 (i) a separate application for approval of the mechanism; or

28 (ii) only with the prior approval of the Commission, an application
29 for a base rate proceeding, including an alternative rate proceeding, or any other proceeding
30 to alter the utility company’s base rates under the authority of the Commission.

31 (2) A proposal submitted under this section shall allocate the prudently
32 incurred costs of the limited–income mechanism across rate classes.

33 (3) The proposal shall include:

34 (i) a detailed description of the proposed mechanism;

35 (ii) the proposed method for allocating the mechanism’s costs across
36 customer classes;

1 (iii) the rationale supporting the utility company’s proposal for a
2 mechanism to benefit the eligible limited–income customers in the utility company’s service
3 territory;

4 (iv) a time frame and process for the Commission to review the
5 effectiveness of the mechanism after implementation; and

6 (v) any other information the Commission considers necessary or
7 useful to evaluate the proposal.

8 (e) In evaluating a limited–income mechanism, the Commission shall consider:

9 (1) the degree to which the mechanism promotes affordability of WATER,
10 SEWAGE DISPOSAL, electricity, or natural gas for limited–income customers;

11 (2) the public interest in allocating the costs of the mechanism between the
12 utility company’s shareholders and rate payers;

13 (3) the impact on rates, utility operating costs, customer arrearages,
14 customer disconnections, uncollectible costs, and successful completion of payment plans;

15 (4) the ability of a limited–income customer to continue to receive benefits
16 when relocating within the same service territory;

17 (5) coordination of benefits under the mechanism with any other public or
18 private assistance that may be available to the customer;

19 (6) a minimum level of support or assistance structure to provide equitable
20 availability of limited–income assistance across the State; and

21 (7) any other information the Commission considers appropriate.

22 (f) If an approved limited–income mechanism requires that the Office of Home
23 Energy Programs must certify an eligible limited–income customer’s qualifications to
24 participate in a limited–income mechanism, the Office shall certify an eligible
25 limited–income customer’s qualifications before the customer may participate in the
26 mechanism.

27 (g) An eligible limited–income customer who participates in a mechanism under
28 this section may also be eligible for other assistance programs offered in the State,
29 including those offered by a utility company or the Office of Home Energy Programs, the
30 Department of Housing and Community Development, or any other public or private
31 source.
