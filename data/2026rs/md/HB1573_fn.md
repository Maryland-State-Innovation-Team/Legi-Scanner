Ways and Means 
 
 
Income Tax - Credit for Small Political Contributions 
 
 
This bill authorizes a refundable individual income tax credit for up to $50 ($100 if married 
filing jointly) of political contributions paid to a campaign finance entity, within the 
meaning of the Election Law Article, during the tax year. The credit may be claimed only 
once. By December 1 annually, the Comptroller must report to the Governor and the 
General Assembly on the number of taxpayers who have claimed the credit and the credit’s 
fiscal impact. The bill takes effect July 1, 2026, applies to tax years 2026 through 2030, 
and terminates June 30, 2032. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  General fund revenues decrease by an indeterminate amount annually in 
FY 2027 through 2031. General fund revenue losses may exceed $6.0 million in FY 2027, 
as discussed below. It is assumed that the Comptroller’s Office can implement the bill with 
existing budgeted resources. 
  
Local Effect:  None. 
 
Small Business Effect:   None. 
 
 
Analysis 
 
Bill Summary:  “Political contribution” means the gift of money to (1) promote or assist 
in the promotion of the success or defeat of a candidate, political party, question, or 
prospective question; or (2) assist in the payment of expenses associated with contesting 
an election under Title 12 of the Election Law Article.

Current Law:  The State does not offer an income tax benefit for political contributions 
but does offer an income tax checkoff for the Fair Campaign Financing Fund on the 
individual income tax return. In general, all campaign finance activity for a State or 
county election must be conducted through a campaign finance entity. 
 
State Revenues:  General fund revenues decrease by an indeterminate amount annually in 
fiscal 2027 through 2031 due to credits claimed against the personal income tax. While a 
precise estimate is not feasible at this time, general fund revenue losses may be significant 
in a given year, particularly in fiscal 2027. Based on a preliminary analysis of 
2022 contribution data (the most recent gubernatorial election year) published by the 
State Board of Elections, the Department of Legislative Services estimates that  
fiscal 2027 general fund revenue losses may exceed $6.0 million. 
 
 
Additional Information 
 
Recent Prior Introductions:  Similar legislation has not been introduced within the last 
three years.
