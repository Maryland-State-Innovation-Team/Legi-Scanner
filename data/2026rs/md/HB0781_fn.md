Public Middle Schools – Instruction on the Consequences of a Conviction for 
Felony Murder 
 
 
This bill requires the State Board of Education (SBE) to develop age-appropriate 
curriculum resources on the consequences of a conviction for felony murder for students 
in grades 6 through 8 in public schools and to determine the appropriate subject area in 
which the instruction must be incorporated. Beginning in the 2027-2028 school year, each 
local board of education must incorporate the instruction based on the curriculum resources 
developed by SBE and ensure that each student receives the instruction at least once during 
grades 6 through 8. The bill takes effect July 1, 2026. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:   General fund expenditures increase by $250,000 only in FY 2027 for 
contractual services to research, develop, and publish age-appropriate curriculum resources 
required by the bill. Revenues are not affected.

Analysis 
 
Bill Summary:  “Consequences of a conviction for felony murder” is defined as the legal 
penalties that take away an individual’s freedom, rights, and access to programs or services 
after a conviction for murder in the first degree under Title 2 of the Criminal Law Article.  
 
SBE may adopt regulations to implement the bill. 
 
Current Law:  With the advice of the State Superintendent of Schools, SBE establishes 
basic policy and guidelines for the program of instruction for public schools. Subject to 
State law and the regulations, bylaws, policies, and guidelines established by SBE, each 
local board of education must establish the curriculum guides and courses of study for 
schools in its jurisdiction. Policies, rules, and regulations for the graduation of students 
from Maryland public schools are established by local boards of education and SBE. 
 
MSDE advises that at the middle school level the State Standards and Frameworks in 
Social Studies do not include the content required by the bill; however, opportunities to 
learn about criminal law and the criminal justice system are directly addressed in 
High School United States History and American Government. 
 
State Expenditures:  The bill requires SBE to develop age-appropriate curriculum 
resources for instruction on the consequences of a conviction for felony murder for students 
in grades 6 through 8. As SBE is generally responsible for developing curriculum standards 
and guidelines, developing curriculum resources (e.g., curriculum guides and instructional 
materials) is beyond its normal expertise. 
  
As a result, MSDE advises that development of these curriculum resources, including 
research, drafting instructional materials, and determining the appropriate subject area for 
incorporation, necessitates hiring a consultant team at an estimated one-time cost of 
$250,000 in fiscal 2027. However, to the extent existing instructional materials or related 
curriculum resources can be adapted, or internal staff capacity can be utilized, the total cost 
may be less. Further, the scope of work may be less than that imagined by MSDE. 
 
No additional consultant costs are anticipated in future years; however, MSDE may incur 
ongoing operational responsibilities associated with providing technical assistance and 
implementation support to local school systems. These ongoing responsibilities are 
assumed to be handled with existing resources. Actual costs may vary depending on the 
scope of the curriculum materials developed and the extent to which internal staff resources 
are leveraged.

SBE. Accordingly, local school system expenditures may increase in fiscal 2027 and 2028 
to review and integrate the State-developed curriculum resources, revise lesson plans, 
provide teacher professional development, update instructional materials, and establish 
processes to track student participation. 
 
Several local school systems anticipate modest one-time curriculum development costs. 
Reported estimates range from approximately $500 (Wicomico County) to $22,500  
(Prince George’s County) for curriculum alignment and instructional planning.  
Anne Arundel County and St. Mary’s County similarly anticipate additional expenditures 
for curriculum development and teacher professional development. By contrast,  
Frederick County and Montgomery County estimate minimal costs, assuming the required 
instruction can be incorporated into existing courses and professional development cycles. 
 
Beginning in fiscal 2028, local school systems incur costs associated with implementing 
the required instruction and monitoring student completion; however, ongoing costs are 
expected to be limited to the extent instruction is embedded within existing coursework 
and no additional staffing is required. Actual costs depend on the scope of the curriculum 
resources developed by SBE. Costs are expected to be absorbable within existing resources 
for most local school systems. 
 
Additional Comments:  In December 2021, the Department of Legislative Services 
produced a report entitled “Collateral Consequences of a Criminal Conviction”.
