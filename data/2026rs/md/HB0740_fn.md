Ways and Means and Health 
 
 
Public Schools - School-Based Mental Health Services - Full-Time Therapist 
 
 
This bill requires each public school in the State to employ a full-time therapist that 
provides school-based mental health services to students during the instructional day. The 
therapist must be licensed and certified by the State Board of Professional Counselors and 
Therapists. The bill takes effect July 1, 2026. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  State general fund expenditures increase beginning in FY 2027 to the extent 
the two State-run public schools (the SEED School of Maryland and the Maryland School 
for the Deaf) are not in compliance with the bill. Revenues are not affected. 
  
Local Effect:  Local school system expenditures increase significantly beginning in 
FY 2027 to hire a full-time therapist at each public school, as discussed below. Local 
revenues are not affected. This bill imposes a mandate on a unit of local government. 
  
Small Business Effect:  None. 
  
 
Analysis 
 
Current Law:  The State Board of Professional Counselors and Therapists licenses and 
certifies professional counselors, alcohol and drug counselors, marriage and family 
therapists, professional art therapists, and behavior analysts; receives and resolves 
complaints; and sets standards for the practice of counseling and therapy.

With the assistance of the local health department (LHD), each local board of education 
must provide adequate school health services, instruction in health education, and a 
healthful school environment. The Maryland State Department of Education (MSDE) and 
the Maryland Department of Health (MDH) must jointly develop public standards and 
guidelines for school health programs and assist local boards of education and LHDs in 
their implementation.  
 
Chapter 36 of 2020, The Blueprint for Maryland’s Future (Blueprint) established the 
Maryland Consortium on Coordinated Community Supports within the Maryland 
Community Health Resources Commission. The consortium must complete certain tasks 
related to the development of coordinated community supports partnerships to meet student 
behavioral health needs and other related challenges in a holistic, nonstigmatized, and 
coordinated manner, including developing a statewide framework for the creation of a 
coordinated community supports partnership, implementing a related grant program, 
evaluating a payment reimbursement program for providers, and developing a program for 
uninsured students. In addition, in consultation with MSDE, the consortium must develop 
best practices for the creation of a positive classroom for all students. The Blueprint also 
expanded funding for school-based health centers and for local school systems to train 
school staff to recognize student behavioral health issues and required local boards of 
education to enhance and expand the availability of behavioral health services to students.  
 
School-based Health Centers 
 
School-based health centers (SBHCs) are health centers located in a school or on a school 
campus that provide on-site comprehensive preventive and primary health services. 
Services may also include behavioral health, oral health, ancillary, and supportive services. 
According to MDH’s fiscal 2024 School-based Health Centers Report, for the  
2023-2024 school year, there were 89 SBHCs operating across 16 local jurisdictions.  
 
School Therapists 
 
MSDE advises that the agency collects staffing information on some school health and 
wellness personnel through the Maryland Report Card. Additionally, MSDE’s Staff and 
Student Publications provide some detail on the types of health and wellness personnel 
employed in public schools, including therapists. 
 
State Expenditures:  General fund expenditures increase beginning in fiscal 2027 to the 
extent that the two State-run public schools (the SEED School of Maryland and the 
Maryland School for the Deaf) do not currently retain a full-time therapist licensed and 
certified by the State Board of Professional Counselors and Therapists to provide  
school-based mental health services during the instructional day.

MSDE advises that, to implement the bill, the department requires a full-time education 
program specialist to implement guidelines and standards for integrating full-time 
therapists into public schools, managing data collection and reporting systems, providing 
professional development, and coordinating with MDH and the State Board of Professional 
Counselors and Therapists. This position would increase MSDE general fund expenditures 
by $99,736 in fiscal 2027, increasing to $134,830 in fiscal 2031. The Department of 
Legislative Services advises that the bill does not require any statewide coordination or 
oversight of therapists in public schools.  
 
Local Expenditures:  Local school system expenditures increase significantly to the extent 
public schools currently do not retain a full-time therapist (with the appropriate licensure 
and certification) to provide school-based mental health services to students during the 
instructional day. MSDE’s Staff and Student Publications identify 2,233 therapists working 
in Maryland public schools as of October 2025. However, many of these therapists may 
currently perform duties other than working full-time to provide school-based mental 
health services during the instructional day. 
 
Anne Arundel County Public Schools advises that it employs some social workers licensed 
by the State Board of Social Work Examiners to provide school-based mental health 
services. Many of these social workers split their days across multiple schools. The bill’s 
requirement for a full-time therapist licensed and certified by the State Board of 
Professional Counselors and Therapists at each school would therefore require the school 
system to hire an additional 55 licensed therapists at a cost of up to $7.0 million annually 
beginning fiscal 2027.  
 
St. Mary’s County Public Schools advises that it employs three licensed therapists. 
Assuming that these therapists provide full-time school-based mental health services, costs 
increase for the school system to hire an additional 25 licensed therapists at a cost of 
approximately $2.9 million in fiscal 2027. 
 
Frederick County Public Schools similarly advises that the cost to hire one full-time 
licensed therapist at each of its public schools could cost $10.0 million annually. 
 
Wicomico County Public Schools advises that its existing school counselors and social 
workers are generally licensed through MSDE, not necessarily the State Board of 
Professional Counselors and Therapists. Thus, the bill would necessitate significant 
additional expenditures to hire therapists to provide coverage at all schools.
