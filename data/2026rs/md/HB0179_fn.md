Department of Commerce - Complaint Portal and Annual Report 
 
 
This bill requires the Department of Commerce to create and maintain a portal on its 
website that allows members of the public to file a complaint if a governmental unit (at the 
State or local level) takes longer than 60 days to process an application for a license, form, 
certificate, certification, permit, or registration for a business or nonprofit organization. 
The bill further requires Commerce, by September 15, 2026, and every September 15 
thereafter, to submit a report on the complaints filed through the portal to specified 
committees of the General Assembly. The bill takes effect July 1, 2026. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  Commerce advises it can handle the bill’s requirements with existing 
budgeted resources. Revenues are not affected. 
 
Local Effect:  The bill does not materially affect local government operations or finances. 
 
Small Business Effect:  Minimal. 
 
 
Analysis 
 
Bill Summary:  The report submitted by Commerce must identify, with respect to each 
complaint received during the preceding fiscal year, the governmental unit responsible for 
the license, form, certificate, certification, permit, or registration. Commerce must make 
reasonable efforts to anonymize the data to protect the privacy of any business or nonprofit 
organization to which a complaint may be related.

A person may not file a complaint using the portal if a State or local law, ordinance, or 
resolution authorizes the governmental unit to take longer than 60 days to process the 
application. 
 
Current Law:  Among other duties and objectives, Commerce must assist the growth and 
revitalization of small businesses; support the growth of the State and regional economies 
by providing consulting, technical assistance, and liaison activities on business and 
economic development issues; promote regulatory reform and coordinate efforts with other 
State and local units; and encourage location and development of new businesses in the 
State and the retention and expansion of present enterprises in coordination with local 
governments and local economic development units. 
 
Additional Comments:  Most of the State agencies that responded to a request for 
information advise that they typically process applications in less than 60 days. 
 
 
Additional Information 
 
Recent Prior Introductions:  Similar legislation has been introduced within the last 
three years. See SB 496 and HB 1038 of 2025. 
 
Designated Cross File:  None.
