Economic Development - Maryland Workforce Launch Pilot Program - 
Establishment 
 
 
This bill establishes the Maryland Workforce Launch Pilot Program in the Department of 
Commerce to provide customized, no-cost workforce training as a business attraction tool 
to recruit strategic industries and qualified business entities to the State. Commerce must 
partner with no more than two community colleges to deliver training services and must 
distribute funds to a partner community college to cover the direct costs of developing and 
delivering the training. The bill specifies that any curriculum, manuals, or instructional 
materials developed under the program are owned by the community college. A related 
reporting requirement is established. The bill terminates September 30, 2029. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  General fund expenditures increase by $1.0 million in FY 2027 and 2028 as 
the bill effectuates a contingent appropriation in the FY 2027 budget passed by the 
General Assembly and discretionary funding is assumed to continue at that level in 
FY 2028. Minimal additional general fund expenditures are maintained for contractual 
staffing in FY 2029. A portion of the funding for FY 2027 and 2028 may be provided to 
Baltimore City Community College (BCCC) if it is a partner community college.

Analysis 
 
Bill Summary: 
 
Definitions  
 
“Qualified business entity” means a business entity that (1) does not have a physical 
location in the State; (2) is in active negotiations with Commerce to establish a location in 
Maryland from outside the State; and (3) enters into an agreement with Commerce to 
participate in the program. “Strategic industry” means an industry sector identified by 
Commerce in the State’s economic strategic plan. 
 
Maryland Workforce Launch Pilot Program – Requirements 
 
For each qualified business entity that receives assistance under the program, Commerce 
must establish performance metrics, including the number of full-time jobs created and the 
salary the entity is providing to trainees. The program agreement must include a provision 
requiring the qualified business entity to reimburse Commerce for the cost of the training 
services if the entity fails to meet the established performance metrics. 
 
By December 31, 2028, Commerce must submit a report to the Governor and the 
General Assembly on the effectiveness of the program, including (1) revenue generated by 
the qualified business entities; (2) jobs created; and (3) recommendations for statewide 
expansion. 
 
Current Law:  The Partnership for Workforce Quality (PWQ) Program, administered by 
Commerce, has a similar purpose – specifically to provide training services to:  
 
 
improve the competitiveness and productivity of the State’s workforce and business 
community; 
 
upgrade employee skills, or train new employees, for new technologies or 
production processes; and 
 
assist employers located in the State in promoting employment stability. 
 
Eligibility 
 
In order to receive assistance under the program, a Maryland-based employer must request 
training assistance in job-specific skills to train new employees or upgrade or retain

Business Assistance Services 
 
The program must provide business assistance services that, among other things, 
(1) determine whether the employer’s specific needs are best met by training, other types 
of assistance, or a combination of services; (2) identify or develop appropriate curricula; 
and (3) determine the most cost-effective approach to meeting training needs. The program 
may provide business assistance services at no cost to the employer. 
 
Training 
 
Grants may be awarded for job-specific training assistance to an eligible business, 
community college, private career school, State-accredited training agency, trade 
association, or union-sponsored training program. Training must be approved by the 
employer, and job-specific training may not exceed one year. 
 
Partnership for Workforce Quality Fund 
 
The PWQ Fund is a special fund managed and supervised by the Secretary of Commerce. 
The fund consists of money appropriated in the State budget to the fund, money made 
available to the fund through federal programs, private contributions, an application or 
other fee paid to the program in connection with processing a request for financial 
assistance, and any other money made available to the fund. Investment earnings are 
credited to the fund.  
 
Commerce may use money in the fund for grants to defray the cost of workforce training 
and administrative, actuarial, legal, and technical services for the program. 
 
The Governor must include in the State budget for each fiscal year an appropriation of  
at least $1.0 million for the program. 
 
Use of Program Money 
 
Program money may be used for costs associated with the direct delivery of instruction, 
including curriculum development, course materials, and instructors’ salaries and expenses 
for training. 
 
At least 60% of the money available to the program must be reserved for employers with 
150 or fewer employees based in the State. Up to 20% of available program funding may

State/Local Fiscal Effect: 
 
Commerce 
 
The fiscal 2027 budget as passed by the General Assembly includes $1.0 million in 
general funds for the Maryland Workforce Launch Pilot Program in Commerce, contingent 
on the enactment of this bill. Therefore, general fund expenditures for Commerce increase 
by $1.0 million in fiscal 2027. Although funding for the program in fiscal 2028 is 
discretionary, this analysis assumes that $1.0 million is also provided in fiscal 2028 to 
continue supporting training grants and staffing costs; general fund expenditures are also 
assumed to increase minimally in fiscal 2029, as discussed below.  
 
Commerce advises that, as recent staff retirements have reduced the agency’s capacity to 
administer additional programs, a new position to manage and oversee the program is 
necessary. Thus, a portion of the funding provided – $71,869 in fiscal 2027, which accounts 
for the bill’s October 1, 2026 effective date, and at least $81,731 in fiscal 2028 – is expected 
to be used to hire one contractual full-time finance specialist within the Office of Finance 
Programs to administer the program. This estimate includes a salary, fringe benefits, 
one-time start-up costs, and ongoing operating expenses.  
 
Residual funding is expended as training grants to partner community colleges to support 
development and delivery of customized workforce training for participating businesses. 
Based on costs observed in the PWQ Program (only half of which is paid by the State), 
training provided under the bill costs approximately $6,000 per trainee. Therefore, funding 
available to community colleges for training, estimated at $928,131 in fiscal 2027 and 
$918,269 in fiscal 2028, is sufficient to support approximately 150 trainees each year. 
Actual expenditures under the pilot program depend on the level of funding provided in 
fiscal 2028 (or even fiscal 2029) and the number of participating businesses and trainees.

This estimate assumes that the full-time contractual finance specialist remains on board 
through the first half of fiscal 2029 to handle any remaining administrative duties. 
Therefore, the fiscal 2029 estimate ($42,617) reflects a salary with annual increases and 
employee turnover, annual increases in ongoing operating expenses, and termination of the 
contractual position on December 31, 2028, when the report analyzing the effectiveness of 
the program must be completed. 
 
The estimate assumes there is no funding for partner community colleges in fiscal 2029, as 
that year is assumed to be used to analyze the effectives of the funding already distributed. 
However, the timing of program implementation is uncertain, as it is not known how 
quickly Commerce can establish relationships with qualified business entities outside the 
State or partner community colleges. Further, the bill does not preclude funding for the 
pilot program in fiscal 2029. 
 
The estimate does not include any health insurance costs that could be incurred for 
specified contractual employees under the State’s implementation of the federal Patient 
Protection and Affordable Care Act. 
 
The estimate also does not reflect any potential reimbursements to Commerce from 
qualified business entities for the cost of the training services if any such entities fail to 
meet the performance metrics established under the bill. 
 
Baltimore City Community College and Local Community Colleges 
 
BCCC and local community college revenues and expenditures increase to the extent 
community colleges are selected as program partners and receive State funding to provide 
training services. However, most community colleges are not affected as the bill requires 
Commerce to partner with no more than two community colleges to deliver training 
services (as discussed in Additional Comments below). The magnitude of the fiscal impact 
on the partner community colleges depends on participation levels and whether State 
funding fully covers training costs. However, under one set of assumptions, a selected 
community college’s revenues and expenditures may increase by approximately 
$6,000 per trainee. If one community college receives one-half of the assumed annual 
funding for the pilot program, that college’s revenues and expenditures increase by 
approximately $460,000 in that year. 
 
Small Business Effect:  The pilot program is for businesses outside of Maryland that 
choose to relocate to the State; therefore, it does not affect existing small businesses. 
Nevertheless, to the extent the program successfully attracts new business entities, small 
businesses in the same geographic area may benefit from an increase in economic activity.
