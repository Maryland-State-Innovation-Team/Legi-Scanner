Modernizing Civil Relief for Service Members Act 
 
 
This bill explicitly authorizes an active service member or an active service member’s 
spouse who relocates to the State due to orders for service and holds a license issued by 
another state to practice under that license within Maryland if the individual submits an 
application including specified documentation and satisfies other criteria. If an active 
service member or spouse is authorized to practice under the bill’s provisions, the 
individual may practice only in the discipline and scope of practice allowed by the license 
in the other state, and the licensing authority in the State may not require another license 
unless the individual seeks to practice outside of the authorized discipline or scope of 
practice. An active service member or spouse authorized to practice under the bill (1) may 
practice to the same extent and capacity as an individual licensed in the State, including 
supervision of a licensed individual required to practice under supervision, and (2) is 
subject to the same disciplinary authority as an individual licensed in the State. Each 
licensing authority may adopt regulations to carry out the bill. The bill also makes 
conforming changes and modifies the eligibility requirements for certain expedited 
licensing procedures.

Analysis 
 
Bill Summary/Current Law: 
 
Authorization to Practice Under the Bill for Active Service Members and Spouses 
 
Definitions:  “Active service member” means an individual who is (1) an active-duty 
member of the uniformed services or (2) serving in a reserve component of the uniformed 
services on active-duty orders. “Licensing authority” means an agency of the State that 
issues a license. “License” means a license, permit, certification, registration, or other legal 
authorization required for engaging in an occupation or a profession. 
 
Application Process:  An application to practice under the authorization in the bill must 
include: 
 
 
proof of the orders; 
 
for a spouse of a service member, a copy of the marriage certificate; and 
 
a notarized affidavit affirming under penalty of law that (1) the applicant is the 
individual described in the application; (2) all statements in the application are true, 
correct, and complete; (3) the applicant read, understands, meets, and will comply 
with the requirements for licensure and scope of practice in the State; and (4) the 
applicant is in good standing in all states in which the applicant holds or has held a 
license. 
 
A qualifying applicant under the bill must also (1) be in good standing with the regulatory 
authority that issued the license; (2) not be the subject of any disciplinary action (including 
revocation) taken by a regulatory authority against the applicant regarding the license; 
(3) not be the subject of a pending investigation relating to unprofessional conduct; and 
(4) not have voluntarily surrendered the license while under investigation for 
unprofessional conduct by a regulatory authority. 
 
If the licensing authority cannot review the application within 30 days of receiving it, the 
licensing authority may issue a temporary license to the applicant that confers the same 
rights, responsibilities, and privileges as a permanent license while review is pending.  
 
A licensing authority that receives an application may conduct a criminal history records 
check before authorizing the applicant to practice in the State or issuing a temporary license.

Authorizations and Licensure – In General 
 
Generally, under current law, an individual must be licensed by the appropriate licensing 
authority before practicing specified occupations in the State. For certain occupations, there 
are pathways to practicing in Maryland more readily, including through interstate 
compacts, licensure by reciprocity, and expedited licensure for veterans. 
 
Licensing compacts are legally binding agreements between two or more states that allow 
providers licensed in one state to practice in another state without requiring an additional 
license. As of November 2025, the State has entered eight health occupations licensing 
compacts:  the Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology Interstate Compact; the Nurse 
Licensure Compact; the Interstate Occupational Therapy Licensure Compact; the Interstate 
Physical Therapy Licensure Compact; the Interstate Medical Licensure Compact; the 
Counseling Compact; the Psychology Interjurisdictional Compact; and the Social Work 
Licensure Compact. 
 
Section 1-704 of the Health Occupations Article requires each health occupations board to 
expedite the licensure, certification, or registration of an active service member, eligible 
veteran, or eligible spouse. Moreover, a board must assign to each such applicant an advisor 
to assist with the application process. If an active service member, eligible veteran, or 
eligible spouse meets the requirements for licensure, certification, or registration, a board 
must issue the license, certificate, or registration within 15 business days after receiving a 
completed application. If a board determines that an active service member, eligible 
veteran, or eligible spouse does not meet the education, training, or experience 
requirements for licensure, certification, or registration, a representative of the board must 
assist the applicant in identifying programs that offer relevant education or training or ways 
of obtaining needed experience. Section 2.5-106 of the Business Regulation Article, 
Section 6-101.1 of the Education Article, and Section 11-612.2 of the Financial Institutions 
Article contain similar provisions for other licenses, including educator and mortgage loan 
originator licenses. 
 
Additionally, Maryland Rule 19-219 “Special Authorization for Military Spouse 
Attorneys” authorizes a military spouse to practice law in the State under direct supervision 
of a member of the Maryland Bar if the military spouse is in the State due to military orders. 
 
The bill does not apply to an occupation or profession for which an individual may practice 
in the State under an interstate compact. Additionally, under the bill, licensing authorities 
that offer licensure by reciprocity or expedited licensure pathways for veterans may not 
require an authorized individual to apply for license by reciprocity or expedited licensure.

Other Provisions 
 
Although there are specified exceptions, under § 10‐206 of the Business Occupations and 
Professions Article, an individual must generally be admitted to the State Bar (and meet 
any other requirements that the Supreme Court of Maryland may set by rule) before 
practicing law in the State. The bill adds that the requirements of § 10‐206 are not 
applicable to an individual authorized to practice law in the State by the Supreme Court of 
Maryland.  
 
Under current law, the Automotive Safety Enforcement Division of the Department of 
State Police must administer an examination to each inspection mechanic applicant. The 
bill alters that provision such that an individual authorized to conduct vehicle inspections 
under the bill must not be required to take the examination. 
 
Under current law, to qualify for expedited educator, mortgage loan originator, or health 
occupations licensure with veteran status, an individual must be one of the following:

The bill also updates references to the U.S. Code for revisions to the Servicemembers Civil 
Relief Act (SCRA). 
 
Servicemembers Civil Relief Act 
 
Recent changes to the SCRA allow for professional license portability for active service 
members and the spouses of active service members. In general, the provisions in the 
federal law regarding the application process mirror the procedures established in the bill. 
 
State Fiscal Effect:  As discussed below, several licensing authorities in the State 
anticipate additional expenditures for personnel and/or programming changes to 
implement the bill, in addition to reduced revenues from licensing fees. The Department 
of Legislative Services advises, however, that because many of the changes in the bill 
codify the SCRA, some of these fiscal effects may not be directly attributable to the bill, 
but instead to federal requirements. 
 
Maryland Department of Agriculture 
 
The Maryland Department of Agriculture advises that most of the licenses for which the 
bill applies already have no-fee interstate reciprocity, with the exception of veterinarians. 
The State Board of Veterinary Medical Examiners (SBVME) estimates that no more than 
10 active service members and/or spouses will seek licensure as a veterinarian under the 
bill, and current license fees are $300 annually. Therefore, special fund revenues may 
decrease by up to $3,000 on an annual basis. 
 
SBVME advises that the bill may require modifications to its licensure software to 
accommodate the new authorization. Based on an estimate from similar projects, 
special fund expenditures may increase by at least $15,000 in fiscal 2027 for a one-time 
software update.

Maryland State Department of Education 
 
The Maryland State Department of Education (MSDE) advises that the expanded definition 
of “eligible veteran” and “eligible spouse” may significantly increase the number of 
individuals eligible for an expedited license. For context, the department received 
487 military designated applications in calendar 2025. Of those applications, 288 were for 
initial licensure, 49 were for reinstatement, and 150 were for renewal. 
 
To identify eligible veterans, active service members, and their spouses, and ensure the 
requirements for authorization under the bill are met by applicants, MSDE advises that it 
needs to hire one part-time administrative aide. MSDE also requires updates to the teacher 
licensure platform to create an expedited authorization process for active service members 
and their spouses eligible under the bill; a one-time cost of $30,000 in fiscal 2027 is 
anticipated. 
 
Therefore, MSDE general fund expenditures increase by $67,429 in fiscal 2027, which 
accounts for the bill’s October 1, 2026 effective date. This estimate reflects the cost of 
hiring one part-time administrative aide to assist in processing military applications. It 
includes a salary, fringe benefits, one-time start-up costs, contractual services to update the 
licensing platform, and ongoing operating expenses.

Maryland Department of Health 
 
The Maryland Department of Health did not respond to a request for information on 
the fiscal and operational impact of the bill. It is generally assumed that special fund

expenditures may also be incurred to the extent that more individuals qualify for the

eligibility. Additionally, special fund revenues may decrease to the extent that boards 
experience decreased revenues from licensing fees beginning in fiscal 2027.

Judiciary 
 
The Judiciary advises that the bill necessitates programming changes to its elicensing 
system to account for the new authorization process. Accordingly, general fund 
expenditures increase by $12,709 in fiscal 2027 only. 
 
Additionally, certain business licenses are issued by the clerks of the circuit courts, with 
license fee revenues subsequently distributed to the general fund and the municipal 
corporation/county where the licensed business is located, as further specified in statute. 
To the extent that licensing fees are not collected due to the bill’s authorization process, 
State general fund revenues and revenues to local jurisdictions are reduced. 
 
Maryland Department of Labor 
 
The Maryland Department of Labor (MD Labor) advises that the Maryland Home 
Improvement Commission (MHIC) and Maryland Real Estate Commission (MREC) need 
to update their licensing systems to implement the bill. Accordingly, a one-time cost of 
approximately $12,792 is anticipated in fiscal 2027 only. Additional costs may be incurred 
for the Office of Financial Regulation. 
 
MHIC and MREC also advise that those individuals authorized to practice in the State 
under the bill’s provisions would likely not contribute to the Guaranty Fund (an additional 
fee for the fund is assessed at the time of license application and renewal); accordingly, 
MD Labor special fund revenues may decrease beginning in fiscal 2027. 
 
Other units of MD Labor can update regulations and handle additional applications using 
existing budgeted resources. 
 
Other Agencies 
 
Other agencies impacted by the bill’s changes generally anticipate being able to implement 
the bill using existing budgeted resources. State revenues associated with other licenses are 
anticipated to decrease, at least minimally, to the extent that individuals take advantage of 
the special procedures under the bill to practice under licenses issued in other states. 
 
Small Business Effect:  Some active service members and their spouses who require 
professional licenses to work for certain small businesses in the State may benefit from the 
authorization process under the bill, as well as the expanded eligibility for expedited 
licensing procedures under current law. Small business employers may similarly benefit. 
However, any meaningful benefit is likely attributable to the federal changes to SCRA.
