Ways and Means 
 
 
Homeschool Students in Maryland - Right to Play 
 
 
This bill authorizes a public high school in the State to allow an eligible homeschool 
student to participate in interscholastic athletics sponsored by the school to the same extent 
that a student who is enrolled in the public school may participate. This authorization 
applies to interscholastic athletics in which a student may participate outside regular school 
hours and for which a student does not earn academic credit. A public high school must 
give placement priority to a student enrolled in the school if there are a limited number of 
spaces available for students in a given athletic activity. A homeschool student who 
participates in an athletic activity sponsored by a public high school is responsible for 
transportation to and from the school, and is subject to (1) the same insurance, health, and 
immunization requirement and exemptions as a student enrolled in the school and  
(2) except for academic rules, the same codes of conduct and safety rules as a student 
enrolled in the school. The bill takes effect July 1, 2026. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  The Maryland State Department of Education (MSDE) can develop any 
regulations using existing resources. Revenues are not affected. 
 
Local Effect:  None. Authorizing, but not requiring, a public high school to allow 
homeschool students to participate in athletic activities does not affect local school 
finances. It is assumed that under the bill, the total number of students participating in 
public school interscholastic athletic activities is not expanded. 
 
Small Business Effect:  None.

Analysis 
 
Bill Summary:  A “homeschool student” is a student who participates in a home 
instruction program that meets the requirements of State regulations. It does not include a 
student participating in a home instruction program that is undergoing a deficiency 
correction. 
 
If allowed by the relevant high school, a homeschool student may (1) participate in 
interscholastic athletics at the public high school that the student would be assigned to;  
(2) if living in an area that does not assign students to a specific school, participate at a 
public high school that is proximate with the student’s residence, as specified; or (3) if the 
aforementioned schools do not offer the interscholastic athletics the student seeks, 
participate in another public school that does.  
 
If a public high school has liability insurance for interscholastic athletics, that insurance 
must cover homeschool students equally with enrolled students. To the extent that a public 
high school requires enrolled students to pay insurance premiums, sign waivers, or show 
proof of liability insurance, the high school must require the same of the homeschool 
participant.  
 
Current Law/Background:  State regulations under MSDE govern the athletic program 
for all high school students in Maryland public secondary schools that are members of the 
Maryland Public Secondary Schools Athletic Association (MPSSAA), which includes all 
24 school systems. Local school systems may adopt rules governing their athletic programs 
that are more restrictive than those of MPSSAA; less restrictive rules may not be adopted. 
 
Per State regulations, to be eligible for participation in high school level interscholastic 
athletics, students must be officially registered and attending the MPSSAA member school 
they are authorized to attend under regulations of the local school system. They may 
represent only the school in which they are registered and at which it is anticipated they 
will complete their graduation requirements. Each local school system must establish 
standards of participation that assure that students involved in interscholastic athletics are 
making satisfactory progress toward graduation. 
 
Failure to comply with regulations constitutes a violation that results in specified penalties 
against the MPSSAA member school and either the student or the coach or both. 
Depending upon the severity of the violation, more than one penalty may be imposed 
against the MPSSAA member school, student, coach, or all of the above.
