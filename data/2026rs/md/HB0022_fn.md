Correctional Services - Incarcerated Individuals - Identification Cards, Driver's 
Licenses, and Birth Certificates 
 
 
This bill alters existing requirements – and establishes new requirements – related to 
identification documents (i.e., birth certificates, Social Security cards, and driver’s licenses 
or valid photo identification (ID) cards issued by the Motor Vehicle Administration 
(MVA)) of incarcerated individuals sentenced to the Division of Correction (DOC). The 
bill’s provisions may not be construed in a manner that in any way delays the release from 
confinement of an incarcerated individual. The provisions of the bill – and existing related 
requirements – apply only to an incarcerated individual serving a sentence of 18 months or 
more at a State correctional facility. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  The bill’s changes are not anticipated to materially affect State operations or 
finances. 
 
Local Effect:  None. 
 
Small Business Effect:  None. 
 
 
Analysis

MVA-issued photo ID card. The managing official of a State correctional facility is

Requirements Before Release 
 
Confirmation of Required Documents:  At least six months before release, the 
Commissioner of Correction must confirm whether the required documents were obtained. 
As soon as reasonably practicable after that confirmation, the Commissioner of Correction 
and the managing official of a correctional facility must provide the appropriate forms to 
and work with the incarcerated individual to ensure that an MVA-issued photo ID card or 
a driver’s license is available to the incarcerated individual at the time of release from 
confinement. (This requirement may be satisfied by the return of a valid unexpired ID card 
or driver’s license to an incarcerated individual before release from confinement.) 
However, the requirement does not apply if the Commissioner of Correction has 
determined that the incarcerated individual is in possession of an unexpired MVA-issued 
photo ID card or driver’s license. 
 
At least three months before release, the Commissioner of Correction must confirm that 
the individual will be in possession of all required identification documents on release. 
 
Return and Renewal of Documents:  In general, if an incarcerated individual entered a State 
correctional facility with an identification document, the facility must return each 
document at or before the individual’s release. However, the Commissioner of Correction 
and the managing official may not return an expired driver’s license or MVA-issued 
photo ID to an incarcerated individual and must work with the incarcerated individual, 
before release, to renew: 
 
 
the driver’s license if (1) the driver’s license possessed at intake expired less than 
one year before the individual’s anticipated release date; (2) the license has not been 
refused, revoked, suspended, or canceled under any provision of the Maryland 
Vehicle Law; and (3) the license is not subject to a restriction imposed by MVA; or 
 
the photo ID if (1) the driver’s license possessed at intake expired one year or more 
before the individual’s anticipated release date or (2) the MVA-issued photo ID 
possessed at intake expires before the individual’s anticipated release date. 
 
Alterations and Exceptions to Existing Requirements Relating to Identification Cards, 
Birth Certificates, and Social Security Cards:  The Commissioner of Correction is not 
required to meet the existing requirement to issue an ID card to an incarcerated individual

before release from confinement in a State correctional facility if the commissioner has

The bill alters the existing requirement for the Commissioner of Correction to begin the 
process of obtaining an incarcerated individual’s birth certificate by requiring that the 
commissioner begin the process at least nine months before the anticipated release of an 
incarcerated individual. In addition, the requirement (as altered by the bill) does not apply 
if the commissioner has determined that the incarcerated individual is in possession of the 
individual’s birth certificate. 
 
The existing requirement for the Commissioner of Correction to apply to the Social 
Security Administration to obtain an incarcerated individual’s Social Security card does 
not apply if the commissioner has determined that the incarcerated individual is in 
possession of the incarcerated individual’s Social Security card. 
 
Early Release from Confinement:  If an incarcerated individual is released before the 
anticipated date of release, the Commissioner of Correction must provide all identification 
documents belonging to the incarcerated individual to (1) the incarcerated individual as 
soon as reasonably practicable or (2) the issuing agency. 
 
Current Law:  The Commissioner of Correction must issue an ID card to an incarcerated 
individual before release from confinement in a State correctional facility. The ID card 
must meet the requirements for secondary identification for the purpose of an ID card 
issued by MVA under § 12-301 of the Transportation Article. In addition, pursuant to 
Chapter 514 of 2020, the Commissioner of Correction must obtain an incarcerated 
individual’s birth certificate and Social Security card after taking custody of an 
incarcerated individual (unless the incarcerated individual does not consent). 
 
The Commissioner of Correction must adopt regulations (1) establishing a release plan for 
incarcerated individuals upon release from confinement in a State correctional facility to 
help identify resources to assist incarcerated individuals following release, including the 
provision of transportation from the facility for an incarcerated individual upon release and 
(2) implementing provisions concerning the issuance of an ID card to incarcerated 
individuals on release from confinement in a State correctional facility. 
 
Additional Comments:  The Department of Public Safety and Correctional Services 
(DPSCS) advises that, through an interagency agreement with MVA, it has established a 
procedure for assisting incarcerated individuals with the process of obtaining MVA-issued 
ID cards prior to their release from State correctional facilities. DPSCS notes that before 
an MVA-issued ID card can be requested, an incarcerated individual must have both their 
birth certificate and Social Security card. DPSCS further reports that it has a memorandum
