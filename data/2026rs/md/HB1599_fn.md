Chesapeake Bay - Enhancement Program and Maryland Seafood Industry 
Financial Assistance Fund - Established 
 
 
This bill establishes (1) a Chesapeake Bay Enhancement Program in the Department of 
Natural Resources (DNR); (2) a Maryland Seafood Industry Financial Assistance Fund, 
administered by the Maryland Agricultural and Resource-Based Industry Development 
Corporation (MARBIDCO); (3) an authorization for specified MARBIDCO funding to be 
transferred to the financial assistance fund (subject to a repayment requirement); and (4) a 
specified process to help ensure borrower repayment of MARBIDCO loans. The financial 
assistance fund, transfer authorization, and repayment assurance provisions take 
effect July 1, 2026. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  General fund expenditures increase by $2.0 million in FY 2027 only; the  
FY 2027 budget as passed by the General Assembly includes a $2.0 million general fund 
appropriation for the financial assistance fund contingent on the enactment of this bill or 
its cross file. Nonbudgeted revenues increase by $2.0 million, and nonbudgeted 
expenditures increase by $2.7 million, in FY 2027. Nonbudgeted revenues and 
expenditures increase by indeterminate amounts in future years. This analysis assumes that 
the establishment of the Chesapeake Bay Enhancement Program in statute codifies an 
existing program (as discussed below) and is not expected to affect State finances.

Analysis 
 
Bill Summary:   
 
Chesapeake Bay Enhancement Program 
 
The bill establishes a Chesapeake Bay Enhancement Program in DNR to provide funding 
for oyster propagation and replenishment projects that mitigate the effects of transportation 
projects on the State’s oyster population. DNR must use funding under the program to 
support oyster propagation and replenishment and program management. 
 
Maryland Seafood Industry Financial Assistance Fund 
 
The bill establishes a Maryland Seafood Industry Financial Assistance Fund, administered 
by MARBIDCO, to provide financial assistance, including loans, to commercial watermen 
and small businesses in the seafood industry. The fund may be used only for (1) financial 
assistance, including loans, and (2) the costs of MARBIDCO to administer the fund. 
 
The fund consists of (1) money appropriated in the State budget to the fund; (2) loan 
principal repayments related to the financial assistance; (3) money made available from the 
federal, State, or local government; and (4) any other money from any other source 
accepted for the benefit of the fund. 
 
The bill also authorizes MARBIDCO to transfer up to $337,500 each from the Oyster 
Shucking House Loan Program and the Maryland Watermen’s Microloan Program to the 
fund to provide additional financial assistance to commercial watermen and small 
businesses in the seafood industry. MARBIDCO must repay, by June 30, 2028, any funds 
transferred from the programs to the fund. 
 
For financial assistance, including loans, provided from the fund, MARBIDCO must 
determine (1) the application and award process; (2) eligibility criteria; (3) the amount of 
the financial assistance, including loans, for each applicant; (4) the terms and conditions of 
financial assistance, including loans; (5) the amount of loan forgiveness that MARBIDCO 
may provide for loan repayment performance; and (6) any other provisions necessary to 
carry out the provisions governing the fund.

Maryland Agricultural and Resource-Based Industry Development Corporation – Loan 
Repayment Assurance 
 
The bill requires MARBIDCO to notify DNR (1) if a borrower who holds a tidal fish 
license is delinquent in the repayment of a loan to the corporation and (2) if such a borrower 
becomes compliant with the repayment of a loan. After receiving a notification that a tidal 
fish licensee is delinquent in the repayment of a loan, DNR may not approve a transfer of 
the tidal fish license or authorization until receiving a notification that the borrower has 
become compliant with the repayment of the loan. 
 
Current Law:   
 
Oyster Propagation and Replenishment 
 
Statute requires DNR to take measures, which in its judgment seem best calculated to 
increase the productivity or utility of oyster resources in the State, including (1) identifying 
and using effective methods of cleaning diseased oyster bars; (2) providing clean shell for 
the bars; (3) using hatchery produced oysters to replant sites; and (4) applying for a permit 
to dredge buried oyster shells.  
 
DNR and the Maryland Department of Transportation (MDOT) indicate that they have had 
a formal agreement for approximately 30 years under which MDOT provides funding to 
DNR for oyster recovery/reseeding efforts – titled (in its current form) the Agreement 
Between the Maryland Department of Transportation and the Maryland Department of 
Natural Resources for the Chesapeake Bay Enhancement Program (Oyster Recovery).  
 
The agreement has been modified and extended numerous times – most recently in  
April 2022, which extended the term of the agreement through June 30, 2026, and included 
the following language:   
 
“MDOT shall pay to DNR the amount of two million dollars ($2,000,000) 
annually. These funds shall be used by DNR solely for the activities that 
support oyster recovery within the Maryland Waters of the Chesapeake Bay. 
To the greatest extent practicable DNR shall ensure that no less than  
nine hundred twenty-five thousand dollars ($925,000) shall be spent annually 
on oyster propagation and replenishment efforts through 2027. DNR shall 
coordinate annually with the county oyster committees and Maryland oyster 
harvesters on the enhanced funding for the public fishery that may be spent 
on oyster replenishment plantings, activities, and projects.”

Maryland Agricultural and Resource-based Industry Development Corporation 
 
MARBIDCO is a quasi-governmental economic development organization chartered by 
the State to support the State’s agricultural and resource-based industries. MARBIDCO 
manages its finances independent of the State Treasury and its employees are not State 
employees. The corporation administers several existing programs relating to the seafood 
industry: 
 
 
the Maryland Watermen’s Microloan Program, which provides loans to (1) qualified 
commercial fishermen to continue commercial operations in the State, including for 
purchasing boats, mechanical equipment, fishing gear, fishing quota, and any other 
item used in commercial seafood harvesting, and (2) seafood processing businesses; 
 
the Maryland Oyster Shucking House Loan Program, which provides loans to 
finance the establishment or expansion of oyster shucking houses that help increase 
the amount of oyster shells retained in the State and returned to the Chesapeake Bay; 
and 
 
two loan programs that support the development of the shellfish aquaculture 
industry in the State. 
 
Tidal Fish License and License/Authorization Transfers 
 
Apart from commercial licenses to fish for Chesapeake Channa and blue and flathead 
catfish, DNR uses a single, commercial license, known as a tidal fish license, which 
authorizes a licensee to engage in each activity indicated on the license. The department 
may issue authorizations for activities listed in statute for which the indicated fee has been 
paid.  
 
Statute allows for specified permanent and temporary transfers of tidal fish licenses and 
authorizations, with DNR’s approval, including (1) the temporary transfer, up to twice per 
license year, of a license or authorization for not more than the remainder of the  
license year and (2) the permanent transfer of a license or authorization from a person who 
has held a valid tidal fish license for at least two years to a person who provides a notarized 
bill of sale for the license or authorization being transferred.

Nonbudgeted Revenues and Expenditures – Fiscal 2027 and Future Years 
 
Nonbudgeted revenues increase by $2.0 million in fiscal 2027, reflecting the receipt of the 
contingent appropriation by the Maryland Seafood Industry Financial Assistance Fund 
(administered by MARBIDCO, a nonbudgeted entity). Nonbudgeted expenditures increase 
by $2.7 million in fiscal 2027 to (1) provide financial assistance (which MARBIDCO 
expects to provide in the form of loans) and (2) cover any costs of administering the 
funding. The $2.7 million in spending reflects spending of the $2.0 million received by the 
fund through the contingent appropriation and a total of $675,000 transferred to the fund 
from the Oyster Shucking House Loan Program and the Maryland Watermen’s Microloan 
Program ($337,500 from each of those programs). The expenditure estimate assumes that: 
 
 
the full authorized amount of $337,500 is transferred to the fund from each program; 
and 
 
those amounts would not otherwise be spent, in the absence of the bill, from the 
time that they are transferred to the financial assistance fund until their repayment 
to the programs by June 30, 2028 (MARBIDCO indicates that the temporary 
transfer of $675,000 from the programs to the financial assistance fund – during 
fiscal 2027 and 2028 – is not expected to affect the programs’ ability to fully meet 
demand for loans under the programs during those fiscal years).  
 
Nonbudgeted revenues and expenditures increase in future years by indeterminate overall 
amounts, reflecting (1) the fund’s receipt of loan repayment revenues; (2) issuance of 
further loans using the repaid funds; (3) in fiscal 2028, the repayment of the $675,000 to 
the Oyster Shucking House Loan Program and the Maryland Watermen’s Microloan 
Program ($337,500 to each program); and (4) any expenditures to cover administrative 
costs. 
 
Chesapeake Bay Enhancement Program 
 
This analysis assumes that the establishment of the Chesapeake Bay Enhancement Program 
in statute codifies the program that (1) has existed, to date, under the agreement between 
DNR and MDOT discussed under Current Law (which terminates June 30, 2026) and  
(2) is expected to be continued pursuant to a new agreement (or memorandum of 
understanding) between the two agencies. DNR and MDOT indicate that they are nearing 
agreement on the funding and other details of the program for fiscal 2027 and future years, 
with funding currently expected to be $2.0 million in fiscal 2027 and $2.25 million 
annually in fiscal 2028 through 2031.

Loan Repayment Assurance 
 
The process established in the bill for MARBIDCO’s notification of DNR regarding a 
delinquent borrower who holds a tidal fish license, and DNR’s nonapproval of any transfer 
of the holder’s license or authorization, can be handled, by both MARBIDCO and DNR, 
with existing resources. The process may contribute to increased or more timely loan 
repayment revenues for MARBIDCO, whether loan repayment revenues of the Maryland 
Seafood Industry Financial Assistance Fund or loan repayments under other MARBIDCO 
programs. 
 
Small Business Effect:  Small business oyster harvesters and/or other seafood industry 
small businesses in the State meaningfully benefit from loans or other financial assistance 
provided through the Maryland Seafood Industry Financial Assistance Fund.
