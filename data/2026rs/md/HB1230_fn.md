Washington Suburban Sanitary Commission - Procurement - Alterations  
PG/MC 102-26 
 
 
This bill expands an existing local, small business preference program in the  
Washington Suburban Sanitary Commission (WSSC) to include additional categories of 
eligible businesses. WSSC may accept as a registered vendor in the program any person 
that is registered as a vendor through a vendor registration process in another jurisdiction 
or that self-certifies as a vendor in accordance with regulations that WSSC adopts. WSSC 
may also adopt regulations to promote workforce engagement and local economic benefits 
within its service district through WSSC contracts. Finally, the bill repeals the name of 
WSSC’s Office of Supplier Diversity and Inclusion and requires WSSC to adopt 
regulations establishing a name for the office.  
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  None. This bill affects only local government operations. 
 
Local Effect:  None. The bill’s changes are procedural in nature and do not directly affect 
local government finances. 
 
Small Business Effect:  Potential meaningful. 
 
 
Analysis 
 
Bill Summary:  “Local business” means a business with (1) a principal place of business 
in Montgomery or Prince George’s County or (2) at least 25% of the employees of the 
business living in Montgomery of Prince George’s County. “Local small business” means 
a business that qualifies as both a local business and a small business.

The bill expands WSSC’s preference program for local, small business enterprises (and 
rebrands it as a business participation program) to include:   
 
 
local businesses; 
 
small businesses; 
 
local small businesses 
 
Maryland-based businesses; 
 
disabled veteran-owned businesses that are local businesses, small businesses, local 
small businesses, or Maryland-based businesses; and 
 
veteran-owned businesses that are local businesses, small businesses, local small 
businesses, or Maryland-based businesses. 
 
The program must give preference to local small businesses and local businesses, and 
WSSC must adopt any other eligibility criteria the commission determines to be necessary 
or appropriate for the promotion of eligible businesses. The provisions of the bill do not 
require a solicitation issued by WSSC to include every category of eligible business. 
 
As a result, the bill repeals requirements for WSSC to adopt specific criteria requiring 
eligible businesses to (1) meet criteria defining a small business under State law and 
regulations and (2) either have their principal place of business be in Montgomery or  
Prince George’s counties or have at least 25% of their employees residing in those counties. 
 
WSSC’s authority to adopt regulations includes establishing definitions and standards for 
contractor good faith efforts regarding (1) local hiring and recruitment; (2) skills training, 
pre-apprenticeship, and apprenticeship participation or support; and (3) data reporting on 
workforce residency or local economic activity. 
 
The provisions of the bill cannot be interpreted to amend, expand, limit, or otherwise affect 
WSSC’s Minority Business Enterprise Program. 
 
Current Law:  The Office of Supplier Diversity and Inclusion within WSSC is required 
to (1) administer WSSC programs created to remedy discrimination against minority 
business enterprises (MBEs) and promote participation by local small business enterprises; 
(2) promote and coordinate WSSC plans, programs, and operations to remedy 
discrimination against MBEs and its effects; (3) promote the use of WSSC, local 
government, and private entities to remedy discrimination against MBEs and its effects; 
and (4) ensure compliance with certified MBE subcontract participation goals. The head 
of the Office is the Director of Supplier Diversity and Inclusion. 
 
WSSC may establish a local small business enterprise program by resolution or by adopting 
regulations, and the Office of Supplier Diversity and Inclusion must administer the 
program. The purpose of the program is to assist small businesses in Montgomery County

and Prince George’s County by establishing a sheltered market or other appropriate 
preference or by facilitating the award of WSSC contracts or procurement contracts for 
goods and services to small businesses in the counties.  
 
WSSC must adopt administrative procedures for conducting the program, as well as 
eligibility criteria for businesses to qualify for the program, including (1) a requirement for 
small businesses to meet the criteria for a “small business” as defined in State regulations; 
(2) a requirement that the business’s principal place of business be in Montgomery County 
of Prince George’s County or that at least 25% of employees of the business reside in either 
of the counties; and (3) any other eligibility criteria WSSC determines to be necessary or 
appropriate for the promotion of small businesses in WSSC’s service area.  
 
A business may qualify as a local small business enterprise under the program without 
regard to the race, ethnicity, or gender of the participants in the business. WSSC must 
establish criteria for graduation from the program for a local small business that it 
determines no longer requires the assistance or benefits offered by the program. WSSC 
must review the eligibility criteria and administrative procedures of the program each year 
to assess their effectiveness in furthering the purpose of the program.  
 
Small Business Effect:  The changes made to the business participation and vendor 
development program allow WSSC to support additional small businesses, particularly 
veteran- and disabled veteran-owned businesses and businesses not located in Montgomery 
and Prince George’s counties, that are ineligible under the current program. 
 
 
Additional Information
