Maryland Medical Assistance Program - Behavior Technicians - Reimbursement 
 
 
This bill requires the Maryland Department of Health (MDH) to provide reimbursement 
for applied behavior analysis (ABA) services provided by a “behavior technician” to 
Medicaid recipients with autism spectrum disorder (ASD). MDH may not require as a 
condition of reimbursement that an individual receive a certification from a credentialing 
entity recognized by MDH to provide ABA services as a behavior technician. However, 
MDH may require a behavior technician to obtain and maintain a current certification 
within 150 days after the individual begins providing ABA services to Medicaid recipients. 
MDH must adopt regulations necessary to carry out the bill. The bill must be construed to 
apply prospectively only. If, on or before June 30, 2027, the U.S. Department of Health 
and Human Services advises or notifies MDH that the bill is inconsistent with specified 
federal law, the bill terminates. MDH must notify the Department of Legislative Services 
within 5 days after receiving such advisement or notice.  
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  MDH can likely conduct administrative oversight of behavior technicians to 
verify certification as permitted under the bill using existing budgeted resources. Revenues 
are not materially affected. 
  
Local Effect:  None. 
 
Small Business Effect:  Potential meaningful.

Analysis 
 
Bill Summary:  “Behavior technician” means an individual who (1) delivers ABA services 
that are provided to individuals with ASD under the supervision of a licensed psychologist, 
a board certified behavior analyst-doctoral (BCBA-D), or a board certified behavior analyst 
(BCBA) and (2) meets the requirements for providing behavior analysis services under 
Medicaid established by MDH in regulation.  
 
“Board certified behavior analyst” means a licensed professional who renders and provides 
direction for ABA services that are provided to individuals with ASD and has obtained a 
BCBA certification. “Board certified behavior analyst-doctoral” means a licensed 
professional who renders and provides direction for ABA treatments that are provided to 
individuals with ASD and has obtained a BCBA certification with a doctoral-level 
credential. 
 
Current Law:  Maryland regulations (COMAR 10.09.28.01) define “applied behavior 
analysis” as the design, implementation, and evaluation of environmental modifications 
using behavioral stimuli and consequences to produce socially significant improvements 
in human behavior. ABA includes the direct observations, measurement, and functional 
analysis of the relations between environment and behavior. 
 
“Registered behavior technician” (RBT) means a paraprofessional certified by BACB who 
delivers ABA services that are provided to participants with ASD under the supervision of 
a licensed psychologist or a licensed behavior analyst.

State Fiscal Effect:  MDH currently provides reimbursement for ABA services delivered 
to Medicaid participants by RBTs certified by BACB. MDH regulations similarly define 
RBTs as personnel supervised by a licensed psychologist, a BCBA-D, or a BCBA. MDH 
currently permits a 90-day grace period for new RBT providers to achieve certification. 
 
The bill permits MDH to require that a behavior technician obtain and maintain a current 
certification from a credentialing entity recognized by MDH within 150 days after 
providing ABA services to Medicaid recipients. This will require ongoing administrative 
oversight to confirm that behavior technicians have obtained the required certification. Any 
increase in Medicaid expenditures (50% general funds, 50% federal funds) associated with

As of February 2026, Maryland had 1,223 certified behavior technicians. Currently, the 
sole prerequisites for a behavior technician to qualify for the 90-day grace period are a 
high school diploma or a General Educational Development certificate. MDH analysis 
indicates that only 28% of behavior technicians attain RBT certification by the conclusion 
of the 90-day grace period. Consequently, a substantial number of providers are receiving 
reimbursement for therapy services in which they lack full training. Increasing the grace 
period to 150 days is likely to exacerbate this challenge. MDH advises that its preference 
is to exclusively enroll fully certified RBTs to ensure an appropriate standard of care and 
streamline the oversight and administrative processes. 
 
Small Business Effect:  Behavior technicians who work for small business health care 
providers may provide ABA services for up to 150 days before obtaining certification.
