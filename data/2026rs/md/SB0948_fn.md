Election Law - Internet-Delivered Absentee Ballots - Information and Receipt 
Limitation 
 
 
This bill requires that the online absentee (mail-in) ballot application (1) include, for a voter 
choosing Internet delivery, a detailed description of the process for obtaining, completing, 
returning, and processing the absentee ballot and (2) require the voter to confirm they 
understand the requirements and agree to accept delivery of the ballot by the Internet. The 
bill also limits a voter on the permanent absentee ballot list to choosing Internet delivery 
for only one election cycle, after which the State Board of Elections (SBE) must notify the 
voter – in the written pre-election communication sent to each voter on the permanent 
absentee ballot list – that the voter must select a different delivery method for the following 
election cycle. This restriction does not apply to uniformed and overseas voters or voters 
using the online ballot marking tool. The bill takes effect January 1, 2027. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  General fund expenditures increase by approximately $208,500 in FY 2027 
and/or 2028, as discussed below. Revenues are not affected. 
  
Local Effect:  Local government expenditures increase, collectively, by $300,000 in 
FY 2027 only. Revenues are not affected. 
 
Small Business Effect:  None.

Permanent Absentee Ballot Status – Internet Ballot Restrictions and Voter Notification 
 
The bill prohibits a voter who requests permanent absentee ballot status from choosing to 
receive an absentee ballot by the Internet for more than one election cycle. However, this 
restriction does not apply to (1) an absent uniformed services voter or an overseas voter as 
defined in the federal Uniformed and Overseas Citizens Absentee Voting Act or (2) a voter 
using the online ballot marking tool. 
 
For each voter on the permanent absentee ballot list who has chosen to receive an absentee 
ballot by Internet, the bill requires that the pre-election written communication sent to the 
voter – for the election following the election cycle for which the voter received an absentee 
ballot by the Internet – require the voter to choose a different method for receiving an 
absentee ballot for the next election cycle. 
 
Current Law: 
 
Absentee (Mail-in) Voting 
 
Under State law, a voter may request an absentee ballot by completing and submitting 
(1) the SBE approved absentee ballot application; (2) a form provided under federal law; 
(3) a written request that includes the voter’s name, residence address, and signature, and 
the address to which the ballot is to be mailed, if different from the residence address; or 
(4) the accessible online absentee ballot application provided by SBE. 
 
If the applicant qualifies to vote by absentee ballot, the local board of elections must 
provide the ballot by one of the following methods requested by the voter:  (1) mail; 
(2) facsimile transmission; (3) the Internet; or (4) by hand during an in-person transaction. 
 
Permanent Absentee Ballot Status 
 
Chapters 56 and 514 of 2021 allow for a voter to request permanent absentee ballot status 
and be placed on a permanent absentee ballot list, in which case a local board of elections 
sends the voter an absentee ballot each time there is an election. 
 
All voters are eligible for permanent absentee ballot status. To request permanent absentee 
ballot status a voter must complete and submit (1) the SBE-approved absentee ballot 
application and indicate on the form that the voter wishes to have permanent absentee ballot

Online Ballot Marking Tool for an Absentee Ballot Sent by the Internet 
 
SBE may provide an accessible optional online ballot marking tool for use by a voter who 
requested to have their absentee ballot sent by the Internet. “Online ballot marking tool” 
includes a system that allows a voter to (1) access a blank ballot through the Internet; 
(2) electronically mark the ballot with the voter’s selections; and (3) print a paper copy of 
the marked ballot for mailing to a local board. “Online ballot marking tool” does not 
include a system that is capable of storing, tabulating, or transmitting votes or voted ballots 
by electronic or electromagnetic means through the Internet. 
 
Communication Before Each Election  
 
During the period beginning 120 days before an election and ending 90 days before the 
election, SBE must send a written communication to each voter who is on the permanent 
absentee ballot list using the method chosen by the voter on their absentee ballot 
application. 
 
The communication must include (1) confirmation that the voter is included on the 
permanent absentee ballot list; (2) the address of the voter; (3) the method by which the 
voter has chosen to receive an absentee ballot; and (4) a statement that the voter must notify 
the local board if anything should be changed. 
 
If SBE is unable to contact a voter using the method of communication chosen by the voter, 
it must attempt to contact the voter using another method. 
 
If the communication is sent by mail, the envelope must include a statement, prominently 
placed, requesting that the recipient return the communication to SBE if the intended 
recipient no longer lives at that address.

Local Fiscal Effect:  Local government expenditures increase, collectively, by 
$300,000 in fiscal 2027 only, for one-time development costs billed to the local boards of

local boards, for modifications in the statewide voter registration system. SBE advises that

choosing an alternative ballot delivery method. 
 
SBE indicates that there is not a feasible way (regardless of cost) within its current voter

ballot by the Internet who use the online ballot marking tool and those who do not, in order 
to implement the exception – for voters using the online ballot marking tool – to the limit 
on the number of election cycles (to one) in which permanent absentee voters may choose 
to receive an absentee ballot by the Internet.
