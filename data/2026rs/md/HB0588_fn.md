Missing and Murdered American Indian and Alaskan Native Women and Girls - 
Day of Awareness and Reporting Requirement 
 
 
This bill requires the Governor to annually proclaim May 5 as Missing and Murdered 
American Indian and Alaskan Native Women and Girls Awareness Day. The bill also 
requires the Department of State Police (DSP) to submit a report to the General Assembly 
by December 1, 2026, as specified. The bill takes effect July 1, 2026. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  None. Commemorating Missing and Murdered American Indian and 
Alaskan Native Women and Girls Awareness Day does not affect State finances. DSP can 
handle the bill’s reporting requirement with existing budgeted resources. 
  
Local Effect:  None. 
  
Small Business Effect:  None.    
  
 
Analysis 
 
Bill Summary:  By December 1, 2026, DSP must report to the General Assembly whether 
State or local law enforcement agencies are sufficiently collecting data on violence against 
American Indian and Alaskan Native individuals in the State. The report must contain any 
policy recommendations for (1) improving data collection methods; (2) improving 
coordination with federal law enforcement agencies; and (3) raising public awareness of 
violence experienced by American Indian and Alaskan Native individuals (particularly 
women and girls).

Current Law:  Twenty official commemorative days, seven months, and one week are 
recognized in State law, as shown in Appendix – Official Commemorative Days and 
Months in Maryland. The Governor also must issue a proclamation each year 
encouraging citizens and other individuals to observe a moment of silence at 3:00 p.m. on

Marylanders who have served and died in the U.S. Armed Forces.
