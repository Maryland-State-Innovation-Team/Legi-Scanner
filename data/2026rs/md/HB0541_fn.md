Ways and Means 
 
 
Public Schools - Restocking Menstrual Hygiene Products - Requirement 
 
 
This bill clarifies that each local board of education must ensure that each public school 
regularly restocks dispensers for menstrual hygiene products. The bill also makes a 
technical change to clarify that current law requirements relating to menstrual hygiene 
product dispensers are applicable to each public middle and high school and each public 
elementary school. The bill takes effect July 1, 2026. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  None. The bill pertains to local boards of education. 
  
Local Effect:  The bill clarifies current law and therefore does not materially affect local 
governmental finances, as discussed below. 
 
Small Business Effect:  None. 
 
 
Analysis 
 
Current Law:  Chapters 705 and 706 of 2021 require each local board of education to 
ensure that each public school provide, at no charge to the students, menstrual  
hygiene products via dispensers in the women’s restrooms at the school. A public  
middle or high school must install menstrual hygiene product dispensers in at least 
two women’s restrooms on or before October 1, 2022, and in all women’s restrooms on or 
before August 1, 2025. A public elementary school must install menstrual hygiene product 
dispensers in at least one restroom on or before October 1, 2022. 
 
Chapters 705 and 706 also required the State to reimburse a local board of education for 
the cost of purchasing and installing dispensers. The Maryland State Department of 
Education advises that the agency has already issued grants to local school systems in 2022 
to cover the cost of such dispensers.

Local Expenditures:  Local boards of education are already required, under  
Chapters 705 and 706, to ensure that each public school provides menstrual hygiene 
products at no cost to students (including installing dispensers in specified locations). To 
the extent local boards are not already regularly restocking dispensers, the bill may have 
an impact on local boards of education to ensure restocking protocols remain in compliance 
with the updated language of the bill. This may entail additional purchase of menstrual 
hygiene products, however, this analysis assumes expenditures associated with additional 
menstrual products for regular restocking can be absorbed using existing resources with 
minimal overall fiscal impact to a local school system. 
 
 
Additional Information 
 
Recent Prior Introductions:  Similar legislation has not been introduced within the last 
three years. 
 
Designated Cross File:  SB 307 (Senators Hettleman and Love) - Education, Energy, and 
the Environment.
