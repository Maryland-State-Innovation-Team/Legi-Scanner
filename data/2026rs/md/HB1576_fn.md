Ways and Means 
 
 
Sales and Use Tax - Electronic Smoking Devices and Vaping Liquid - Maryland 
Pediatric Cancer Fund 
 
 
This bill increases the sales and use tax imposed on the retail sale of electronic smoking 
devices (ESDs) from 20% to 30% of the taxable price (for vaping liquid sold in a container 
containing up to 5 milliliters of vaping liquid, from 60% to 90% of the taxable price). 
Revenues attributable to the increase in tax imposed on the retail sale of these products are 
distributed to the Maryland Pediatric Cancer Fund. The bill takes effect July 1, 2026. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  Special fund revenues for the Maryland Pediatric Cancer Fund within the 
Maryland Department of Health (MDH) increase by an estimated $9.6 million in FY 2027. 
Future years reflect projected growth in taxable sales. General fund expenditures for the 
Comptroller’s Office increase by an estimated $0.1 million in FY 2027 only.

Analysis 
 
Current Law:  “ESD” is defined as a device that can be used to deliver aerosolized or 
vaporized nicotine to an individual inhaling from the device. It includes an electronic 
cigarette, electronic cigar, electronic cigarillo, electronic pipe, or electronic hookah; a vape 
pen; vaping liquid; and any component, part, or accessory of such a device regardless of 
whether it is sold separately, including any substance intended to be aerosolized or 
vaporized during use of the device.  
 
Sales and Use Tax Imposed on Electronic Smoking Devices  
 
Chapter 37 of 2021 increased the sales and use tax imposed on the retail sale of ESDs to 
12% of the taxable price (60% of the taxable price for vaping liquid sold in a container 
containing up to 5 milliliters of vaping liquid). Prior to Chapter 37, the State’s general  
6% sales and use tax rate applied to the sale of these products. The Budget Reconciliation 
and Financing Act of 2024 (Chapter 717) further increased the sales and use tax imposed 
on the retail sale of ESDs (other than vaping liquid sold in a container containing up to  
5 milliliters of vaping liquid) to 20% of the taxable price and required the Comptroller to 
distribute the revenue attributable to the tax increase to the Blueprint for Maryland’s Future 
Fund (BMFF). Remaining sales and use tax revenues from the sale of ESDs accrue to 
BMFF (12.1% for fiscal year 2027 and beyond) and the general fund.  
 
Maryland Pediatric Cancer Fund 
 
The Maryland Pediatric Cancer Fund within MDH is used to provide grants to eligible 
physicians, hospitals, laboratories, educational institutions, and other organizations and 
persons to conduct pediatric cancer research. Grants may not be used to pay for institutional 
overhead expenses, organizational expenses, or charges that are not directly related to 
pediatric cancer research or expenses arising from community-based support services.  
 
The fund consists of money appropriated in the State budget to the fund and any donations 
or other money from any other source accepted for the benefit of the fund. MDH may use 
up to 5% of the amount in the fund each year for certain administrative and promotional 
expenses.  
 
State Revenues:  Maryland Pediatric Cancer Fund revenues increase by an estimated  
$9.6 million in fiscal 2027 and by similar amounts annually thereafter due to the bill’s 
increase in the sales and use tax imposed on the sale of ESDs, the proceeds of which are 
distributed to the fund. 
 
State Expenditures:  General fund expenditures for the Comptroller’s Office increase by 
an estimated $75,000 in fiscal 2027 for one-time programming changes.

Small Business Effect:  Small business ESD retailers are negatively impacted by the 
increased sales and use tax imposed on ESDs to the extent the tax increase reduces overall 
sales of these products.
