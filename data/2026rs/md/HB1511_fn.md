Public Safety - State Clearinghouse for Missing Persons 
 
 
This bill renames the State Clearinghouse for Missing Children, which is operated by the 
Department of State Police (DSP), to be the State Clearinghouse for Missing Persons and 
transfers its statutory provisions from the Family Law Article to the Public Safety Article. 
The bill further establishes that the duties of the State Clearinghouse for Missing Persons 
include (1) the receipt, collection, and distribution of general information and annual 
statistics regarding missing persons and (2) coordination of law enforcement agencies and 
other interested individuals or groups within and outside the State regarding information 
on persons who have disappeared from, or are thought to be located in, the State. Other 
conforming changes are made to reflect the expansion of the clearinghouse to include all 
missing persons, rather than just missing children. In addition, the bill requires DSP to 
study the department’s capacity to report to the National Missing and Unidentified Persons 
System information about persons who have disappeared from, or are thought to be located 
in, Maryland. By December 1, 2026, DSP must report its findings to the Governor and the 
General Assembly. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  DSP can implement the bill using existing budgeted resources. Revenues are 
not affected. 
 
Local Effect:  The bill’s changes can be handled with existing local resources. Local 
revenues are not affected. 
 
Small Business Effect:  None.

Analysis 
 
Current Law:  The State Clearinghouse for Missing Children is responsible for (1) the 
receipt, collection, and distribution of general information and annual statistics regarding 
missing children and (2) coordination of law enforcement agencies and other interested 
persons or groups within and outside the State regarding information on children who have 
disappeared from, or are thought to be located in, the State. 
 
For children who have disappeared from or are thought to be located in the State, the 
State Clearinghouse for Missing Children (1) must publish the names of and relevant 
available information on missing children and annual statistics regarding missing children 
and (2) may establish and maintain a list of organizations and groups that provide volunteer 
search teams or resources relating to missing children. The Secretary of State Police may 
develop, in cooperation with local law enforcement agencies, a plan for voluntary 
fingerprinting programs for children. 
 
Additional Comments:  In February 2026, DSP completed a study on the department’s 
capacity to report to the National Missing and Unidentified Persons System information 
about persons who have disappeared from, or are thought to be located in, Maryland. DSP 
concluded that, while the department has the technical capability to access the National 
Missing and Unidentified Persons System, the logistical, operational, and resource realities 
necessary to rely on the system do not exist. DSP further noted that the system, although 
accessible, is not the most effective or appropriate tool for managing routine missing 
person cases in Maryland.  
 
Although the General Assembly passed similar legislation in the 2025 session  
(House Bill 1116), the Governor vetoed the bill. 
 
 
Additional Information 
 
Recent Prior Introductions:  Similar legislation has been introduced within the last  
three years. See HB 1116 of 2025.
