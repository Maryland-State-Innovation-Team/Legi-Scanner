State Government - State House Trust - Ten Commandments Monument Display 
Act 
 
 
This bill establishes (1) the Ten Commandments Monument Display Act, which requires 
that a suitable monument of the Ten Commandments be placed on the grounds of the 
Maryland State House, and (2) a Commission on the Ten Commandments Monument that, 
among other things, must develop and implement a process for a monument of the 
Ten Commandments to be designed, constructed, and placed on the Maryland State House 
grounds by a private entity at no cost to the State. The Commission on Artistic Property 
must oversee the care and maintenance of the monument. The bill takes effect 
July 1, 2026. The provisions that establish the commission, and certain related 
provisions, terminate June 30, 2029. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  No effect is assumed in FY 2027. General fund expenditures increase by at 
least $4,000 in FY 2028, and biennially thereafter, for the care and maintenance of the 
monument. Revenues are not affected.

Analysis 
 
Bill Summary:  
 
Suitable Monument of the Ten Commandments 
 
The bill requires a suitable monument of the Ten Commandments to be placed on the 
grounds of the Maryland State House and specifies the text of the Ten Commandments the 
monument must contain. The Commission on Artistic Property must oversee the care and 
maintenance of the monument after it is placed on the grounds of the  
Maryland State House. The bill establishes that these provisions may be cited as the 
Ten Commandments Monument Display Act. 
 
Commission on the Ten Commandments Monument 
 
The bill also establishes a Commission on the Ten Commandments Monument staffed by 
the Division of Historical and Cultural Programs within the Maryland Department of 
Planning in consultation with other appropriate agencies, including the State House Trust, 
the Maryland State Arts Council, and the Department of General Services. The commission 
must:   
 
 
as soon as practicable, but not later than December 31, 2026, develop and implement 
a process for the design, construction, and placement of a monument of the 
Ten Commandments on the grounds of the Maryland State House by a private entity 
at no cost to the State; 
 
advise the Governor, the General Assembly, and State agencies on matters relating 
to the monument;  
 
perform other duties necessary to approve the design and site selection for the 
monument, including arranging for placement of the monument in a location where 
there are no other monuments; and  
 
complete all of its duties by June 30, 2028. 
 
A member of the commission may not receive compensation but is entitled to 
reimbursement for expenses under the standard State travel regulations, as provided in the 
State budget. 
 
In the event that the legality or constitutionality of the monument is challenged in court, 
the Attorney General may (1) prepare and present a legal defense of the monument or 
(2) request the assistance of a nonprofit legal organization that specializes in 
First Amendment cases to prepare and present a legal defense of the monument.

Current Law:     
 
Commission on Artistic Property 
 
There is a Commission on Artistic Property in the Maryland State Archives (MSA). The 
commission is the official custodian of all valuable paintings and other objects of 
decorative art owned by or loaned to the State, except those located in a State room of the 
Government House. The commission is responsible for the acquisition, custody, display, 
location, preservation, proper care, security, and restoration of the objects in its official 
custody. 
 
Each person or agency that desires to acquire a painting or object of decorative art for 
display in or on the premises of any State building, except in a room of the 
Government House, must receive both prior approval and final acceptance from the 
commission. Before giving prior approval or final acceptance, the commission must 
consider (1) the competence of the artist; (2) the proposed location of the object; and (3) the 
quality, historical significance, and appropriateness of the work. 
 
State House Trust 
 
The State House Trust consists of the Governor, the President of the Senate, the Speaker 
of the House of Delegates, the Chairman of the Maryland Historical Trust (or the 
chairman’s designee), and up to three nonvoting associate members with experience and 
interest in historic preservation. The trust is responsible for, among other things, (1) the 
restoration and preservation of the State House and (2) disapproval or approval, and 
supervision, of any proposed repair, improvement, or other change to the State House or to 
any other building within State Circle. 
 
State Expenditures:  General fund expenditures increase for MSA by at least $4,000 in 
fiscal 2028, and biennially thereafter, for the care and maintenance of the monument. This 
estimate reflects minimum costs, without knowing the media from which the monument is 
made, and assumes that (1) the Commission on Artistic Property is responsible for the costs 
of the care and maintenance of the monument; (2) the care and maintenance begin in 
fiscal 2028; and (3) the monument is designed, constructed, and placed on the grounds of 
the Maryland State House by a private entity at no cost to the State. 
 
Small Business Effect:  Any small businesses that are involved in the design, construction, 
and placement of the monument may meaningfully benefit from the bill.
