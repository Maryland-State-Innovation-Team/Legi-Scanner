Vehicle Emissions Inspection Program - Testing and Inspection - Grounds for 
Failure 
 
 
This bill prohibits an emissions testing facility operating under the Vehicle Emissions 
Inspection Program (VEIP) from determining that a motor vehicle has failed to pass a test 
or inspection for any reason other than a determination that:  (1) for an emissions equipment 
and misfuelling inspection, required emissions equipment is not present or the vehicle has 
been misfuelled, or (2) for an exhaust emissions test, the motor vehicle is not in compliance 
with an applicable emissions standard. 
 
 
Fiscal Summary 
 
State Effect:  The bill may conflict with federal law and, as a result, likely has no material 
impact on State finances or operations, as discussed below. However, to the extent that the 
bill can be implemented, operations and finances for the Motor Vehicle Administration 
(MVA) and Department of the Environment (MDE) may be affected, as discussed below. 
 
Local Effect:  None. 
 
Small Business Effect:  None.   
 
 
Analysis 
 
Current Law:  Title 23, Subtitle 2 of the Transportation Article generally requires all 
model year 1977 and newer vehicles in the State to be inspected and tested every two years, 
unless specifically exempt. MVA and MDE must set the VEIP fee for each vehicle to be 
inspected and tested, which may not exceed $30 through June 30, 2026. The current fee is 
$30 but must be adjusted in future fiscal years to account for changes in the

Statutory Waivers/Exemptions 
 
A vehicle owner must be granted a waiver from the VEIP inspection requirement if the 
vehicle fails to pass the exhaust emissions test but the owner provides specified evidence 
of payment for emissions-related repairs. 
 
Persons with special disability registration plates, persons age 70 or older, and deployed 
military personnel are eligible for an exemption from the inspection requirement under 
specified circumstances (MVA/MDE regulations refer to these exemptions as “waivers”). 
 
Any fire or rescue apparatus or ambulance owned or leased by a political subdivision of 
the State, or by a volunteer fire company, rescue squad, or volunteer ambulance company, 
registered as an emergency vehicle is exempt from VEIP inspections. 
 
Regulations 
 
Section 23-207 of the Transportation Article authorizes MVA and the Secretary of the 
Environment to jointly adopt rules and regulations for purposes of implementation, 
administration, regulation, and enforcement of the provisions of the VEIP statute, including 
rules and regulations that, consistent with federal law, exempt certain vehicles from the 
inspections and tests under the program. 
 
MVA/MDE regulations establish various additional exemptions, including exempting 
vehicles not registered in the “emissions inspection area” (Baltimore City and Anne 
Arundel, Baltimore, Calvert, Carroll, Cecil, Charles, Frederick, Harford, Howard, 
Montgomery, Prince George’s, Queen Anne’s, and Washington counties). Diesel vehicles 
are regulated under a separate program. 
 
State Fiscal Effect:  MVA and MDE advise that VEIP testing standards and procedures 
are mandated by the federal Clean Air Act and, pursuant to the Act, VEIP must be 
administered in accordance with applicable U.S. Environmental Protection Agency 
regulations. Because those regulations include specifications for testing procedures and 
pass/fail criteria, the bill’s limitations on the circumstances under which a vehicle may fail 
an emissions inspection likely conflict with these federally required inspection standards. 
Due to the conflict, the bill may not be able to be implemented and, therefore, is anticipated 
to have no impact on State finances or operations.  
 
Nevertheless, if the bill is implemented, State operations and finances may be affected in 
various ways as MVA and MDE modify VEIP and fewer fees are collected from vehicles
